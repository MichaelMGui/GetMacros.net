Clean, separate, cook, chill—and verify

How to Organize a Refrigerator for Food Safety

A tidy refrigerator is useful. A safe refrigerator controls temperature, drips, time, and the path from raw ingredients to ready-to-eat food.

U.S. household guidance: These pages use USDA, FDA, and FoodSafety.gov recommendations. Local rules may differ. Pregnant people, adults 65 and older, children under 5, and people with weakened immune systems face higher foodborne-illness risk and may need stricter choices.

Start with a thermometer

Keep the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below. The control dial is not proof of the actual temperature; use an appliance thermometer in a visible location and check it periodically. Avoid packing shelves so tightly that cold air cannot circulate.

Put raw animal foods below ready-to-eat foods

Raw meat, poultry, and seafood belong in leak-proof containers or sealed bags on the lowest practical shelf. This prevents juices from dripping onto fruit, leftovers, cheese, desserts, or foods that will not be cooked again. The original tray alone may leak.

Do not wash raw poultry. Splashing can spread bacteria around the sink, counters, and nearby food. Cooking to the correct temperature—not rinsing—controls the hazard.

Use the door for foods that tolerate variation

The door warms each time it opens. Keep highly perishable foods in the colder main compartment rather than assuming the egg-shaped door rack is ideal. Condiments and beverages that tolerate modest fluctuation are usually more suitable door residents.

Cool cooked food in shallow portions

Refrigerate perishable leftovers within two hours, or within one hour when air temperature is above 90°F (32°C). Divide a deep pot of soup, chili, rice, or stew into shallow containers so heat leaves faster. Hot food can go directly into the refrigerator in appropriate portions; leaving the whole pot out to “cool completely” extends time in the danger zone.

Create an eat-first zone

Label leftovers with the date refrigerated and place foods that should be used soon where they are visible. Keep a small “eat first” bin for opened items and older produce. Visibility reduces waste without confusing a quality date with a safety guarantee.

Clean spills with purpose

Wipe raw-meat leaks immediately, then clean and sanitize the affected surface. Wash reusable produce drawers and shelves according to appliance instructions. Do not use a strong chemical smell as evidence that the space is safe; correct product concentration and contact time matter.

A weekly five-minute check

  • Read the appliance thermometer.
  • Move raw packages into leak-proof containment.
  • Review dates placed on leftovers.
  • Discard spoiled or time-unknown perishable food.
  • Plan one meal around safe foods approaching a quality limit.
Placement rule: Ready-to-eat food above, raw animal food contained below, leftovers shallow and dated, thermometer visible.
