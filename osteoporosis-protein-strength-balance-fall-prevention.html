Bone health

Osteoporosis Protein, Strength, Balance and Fall Prevention

Fractures depend on both bone strength and whether a fall occurs. Nutrition, muscle, balance, vision, medicines and the home environment work together.

Movement boundary: New fracture, severe pain, marked balance change or recent surgery requires individualized activity guidance rather than a generic exercise program.

Protein supports the structure around bone

Regular protein foods help maintain muscle needed for mobility and fall recovery. Include eggs, dairy or fortified alternatives, fish, poultry, meat, beans, lentils, tofu, nuts or seeds according to preference and medical needs.

Spread opportunities across the day

People with smaller appetites may find it easier to add protein to breakfast and snacks rather than concentrating it at dinner. Kidney disease or another condition can change the plan.

Bone responds to loading

Progressive resistance and weight-bearing activity can support strength and function. Balance practice reduces fall risk. The safest starting point depends on fractures, spinal changes, pain and experience; avoid high-impact or loaded spinal flexion when a clinician has advised against it.

Audit the environment

Lighting, loose rugs, stairs, footwear, pets, bathroom supports and frequently used items can change fall risk. Vision and hearing checks, medication review and assistive devices may matter more than any supplement.

Fuel activity adequately

Under-eating undermines muscle maintenance and recovery. Include carbohydrate for activity, adequate total energy and hydration. A very low body weight or unintentional loss warrants assessment.
