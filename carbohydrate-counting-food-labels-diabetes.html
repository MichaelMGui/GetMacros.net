Carbohydrate Counting and Food Labels

Carbohydrate counting begins with the serving size and total carbohydrate line. Sugar alone does not represent all carbohydrate, and “net carbs” is not the standard value used in every clinical plan.

Do not calculate insulin from this page. Insulin-to-carbohydrate ratios, correction factors and timing are prescriptions. Use the method and targets given by your diabetes clinician or educator.

Start with the portion actually eaten

If a label lists 30 grams of total carbohydrate per serving and you eat one and a half servings, the portion contains 45 grams. Check whether the package is one serving or several; small bags and bottles often contain more than expected.

Total carbohydrate includes several parts

Dietary fibre, total sugars, added sugars and starch are included within total carbohydrate. Do not add sugars to total carbohydrate. Added sugars help compare product quality, but a product with zero added sugar can still contain carbohydrate from flour, milk, fruit or starch.

“Sugar-free” does not mean carbohydrate-free

Sugar-free biscuits, candy or drinks may contain starches or sugar alcohols. Some sugar alcohols can cause gas or diarrhea, and their glucose effect varies. Read the entire panel and follow the counting method your care team recommends.

Recipes require a different method

Add carbohydrate from all ingredients, then divide by the number of portions actually produced. Beans, grains, fruit, milk, sauces and sweeteners may contribute. Restaurant estimates are approximate, so glucose patterns and experience matter.

Fibre supports the overall pattern

Whole grains, beans, vegetables, fruit, nuts and seeds can improve dietary quality and fullness. Increase fibre gradually. Do not subtract fibre automatically unless your individualized education specifically uses a net-carbohydrate method.

Technology does not remove uncertainty

Apps and barcode databases can be outdated or user-entered. Compare app values with the package and use a scale or measuring cup temporarily when learning. The goal is competent estimation, not permanent food surveillance.

When numbers become harmful

If counting causes panic, restriction, bingeing or avoidance of social eating, tell the diabetes team. Alternative approaches can preserve safety while reducing burden, and eating disorders can occur at any body size or diabetes type.
