A missing nutrition value is not zero

Your shelf

Understand unknown nutrient values, incomplete meal totals and filter behavior. Learn why missing fat or sodium must not be recorded as zero.

In this guide

By GetMacros · Published October 3, 2026

“Not verified” means GetMacros does not have a supported number for that nutrient in the listed order. It does not mean the order contains none. Keep that distinction when comparing, filtering or copying a meal into another tool.

For covered U.S. establishments, FDA explains that written nutrient information is available on request. The restaurant’s current source is the next place to check; a missing field in our selection is not proof that the restaurant never publishes it.

Three different kinds of number

DisplayMeaningCan you treat it as complete?
Published 0 gThe source reports zero for its stated serving.Use as published, with normal label limitations.
Known subtotalSome included items have values; others are missing.No. Identify the missing component.
Not verifiedNo supported value for this exact record.No. Do not replace with zero.

One unknown sauce can leave the total unknown

Imagine an entrée with 700 mg sodium and a side with 200 mg. If the included sauce’s sodium is unknown, you know a 900 mg subtotal for the entrée and side. You do not know the whole order’s sodium.

If your maximum is 1,000 mg, that subtotal does not prove the order meets it. The sauce could move the total above the limit. These hypothetical figures demonstrate uncertainty, not a restaurant’s sauce value.

Why a nutrient filter may exclude a meal

A strict protein minimum needs a supported protein value. A strict sodium maximum needs a supported sodium value. An incomplete meal should not pass either test based on a guessed number. Including incomplete records in general browsing does not make their missing nutrients verified.

Similarly, do not reconstruct missing fat by subtracting protein and carbohydrate calories from published calories. Rounded values and different calculation assumptions can make that subtraction unreliable.

What you can do next

  • Open the restaurant source linked with the order.
  • Check that the menu region, size, sauces and included sides match.
  • Choose a record with complete data for the nutrient you need to compare.
  • Report a supported correction with the exact order and source.

Read our data methods and limitations before relying on a comparison for a decision requiring exact values.
