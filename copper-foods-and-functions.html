Copper: iron metabolism, connective tissue, and food sources

Copper is an essential trace mineral involved in energy production, iron metabolism, connective tissue formation, neurotransmitter synthesis, and antioxidant enzymes.

Home › Micronutrient library › Copper: iron metabolism, connective tissue, and food sources

By the GetMacros.net editorial team · Reviewed August 13, 2026 · Educational content, not medical advice

Quick answer: Learn what copper does, which foods supply it, and how high zinc intake can interfere with copper status.

What it does

Copper works through enzymes rather than serving as a direct energy source. True deficiency is uncommon but can occur with malabsorption, some genetic conditions, or excessive zinc supplementation.

Food sources and absorption

Shellfish, seeds, nuts, organ meats, wheat-bran cereals, whole grains, and chocolate can contribute. A varied diet generally provides multiple sources.

Safety and individual context

High zinc intake over time can reduce copper absorption. Both deficiency and excess can be harmful, so isolated high-dose mineral supplementation deserves professional review.

Primary reference

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements consumer fact sheet

How to use this information

Reference values describe population needs and are not a diagnosis. Food patterns, health conditions, pregnancy, laboratory results, and medications can change what is appropriate. Avoid high-dose supplementation unless there is a clear reason and qualified guidance.
