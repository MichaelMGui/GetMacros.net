Migraine, Caffeine, Coffee and Withdrawal

Caffeine can improve some acute headache treatments, trigger attacks in some people, and cause withdrawal when a regular dose suddenly stops.

Medication boundary: Count caffeine in coffee, tea, energy drinks, soda, chocolate and headache medicines. Frequent acute-medicine use can contribute to medication-overuse headache.

Dose varies widely

Cup size, bean, brew time and brand change caffeine substantially. â€œOne coffeeâ€ is not a reproducible dose. Record the product and approximate amount.

Consistency can matter

Large day-to-day swings may be more informative than one beverage. Weekend headaches can follow a later or missed habitual dose, altered sleep or delayed meals.

Taper rather than crash

If reducing caffeine, a gradual plan may lessen withdrawal headache, fatigue and irritability. Replace part of a serving with decaffeinated coffee or reduce measured amounts while keeping other routines steady.

Avoid the rescue loop

Caffeine-containing medicine may help occasionally, but repeated rescue use can complicate headache patterns. Ask a clinician how many acute-treatment days are appropriate for your diagnosis and medicine.

Pregnancy and other conditions change limits

Pregnancy, anxiety, sleep problems, heart rhythm conditions and some medicines can change caffeine guidance. See caffeine and sleep timing.
