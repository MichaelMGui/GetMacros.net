Kidney-health nutrition

CKD Sodium, Fluid and Swelling Guide

Sodium and fluid are related but not identical. Sodium encourages thirst and fluid retention; a fluid limit depends on kidney function, urine output, dialysis, heart health and symptoms.

Urgent assessment: Sudden breathlessness, chest symptoms, confusion, rapidly worsening swelling or very little urine can signal serious fluid or kidney problems.

Count the portion, not the package promise

Check sodium milligrams and serving size. If you eat two servings, double the number. Five percent Daily Value or less is generally low and 20 percent or more high, but a kidney plan may use a personal target.

Find hidden repeat sources

Bread, cured meats, soups, sauces, instant noodles, frozen meals, restaurant dishes and seasoning packets can contribute without tasting intensely salty. Change one frequent source at a time and preserve flavour.

Fluid includes more than water

When a clinician prescribes a limit, beverages, ice, soup, gelatin and foods liquid at room temperature may count. The exact counting rule should come from the dialysis or kidney team.

Manage thirst without unsafe substitutes

Lower sodium can reduce thirst. Small cups, ice counted within the plan, cold fruit allowed by the potassium plan, gum or mouth rinsing may help. Potassium-based salt substitutes require approval.

Daily weight can provide context

Dialysis teams may use weights to estimate fluid gain between treatments. A home scale cannot diagnose the cause of gain, and targets are individualized. Report rapid changes according to the care plan.

Do not use dehydration as treatment

Restricting fluid without instruction can cause dizziness, low blood pressure or kidney injury. Likewise, forcing litres of water does not cleanse CKD. Follow the prescribed range and ask how heat, illness or exercise changes it.

Keep medicines unchanged

Diuretics and blood-pressure medicines affect fluid and electrolytes. Do not change them based on swelling or a meal. Contact the prescriber when symptoms change.
