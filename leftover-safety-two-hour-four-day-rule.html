Leftover Safety: The Two-Hour and Four-Day Rules Explained

The clock starts when perishable food leaves temperature control—not when someone remembers to put a lid on it.

U.S. household guidance: These pages use USDA, FDA, and FoodSafety.gov recommendations. Local rules may differ. Pregnant people, adults 65 and older, children under 5, and people with weakened immune systems face higher foodborne-illness risk and may need stricter choices.

The two-hour rule

Refrigerate perishable food within two hours after cooking or removal from hot or cold holding. When air temperature is above 90°F (32°C), such as at an outdoor picnic, the limit is one hour. Time is cumulative: a casserole that sat through dinner and then rode home has already used part of the window.

Keep the danger zone in view

Bacteria grow rapidly between 40°F and 140°F (4–60°C). Keep cold food at 40°F or below and hot food at 140°F or above while serving. Small platters that are replaced from safe storage work better than continually topping up one room-temperature dish.

Cool quickly, not eventually

Divide soups, stews, rice, meat, and large casseroles into shallow containers. Cut large roasts or poultry into smaller portions. Hot food can be placed directly in the refrigerator in suitable shallow containers; a huge covered pot cools too slowly.

The three-to-four-day refrigerator window

USDA says most cooked leftovers can be kept in the refrigerator for three to four days. Freeze them within that window if they will not be eaten. Freezer time guidance is mainly about quality when food stays continuously frozen at 0°F (-18°C).

Reheat to 165°F

Bring leftovers to 165°F (74°C). Sauces, soups, and gravies should reach a rolling boil. Stir and rotate microwave-heated food, cover it, allow standing time, and check multiple spots. A warm edge does not prove the centre is safe.

Reheating does not erase unsafe storage

Some microorganisms can produce toxins that remain after reheating. If a perishable leftover sat out beyond the limit or its time history is unknown, do not rely on boiling, frying, or microwaving to make it safe.

Label with useful information

Write the dish and refrigeration or freeze date. “Tuesday chili” is more actionable than an unlabelled opaque container. Use the oldest safe food first and avoid combining a fresh batch with an older container, which obscures the clock.

Take-out follows the same rules

Restaurant and delivered food is not exempt. Refrigerate planned leftovers within the same two-hour window, or one hour above 90°F. Bring an insulated cooler when a long trip home makes that impossible.

When in doubt about time or temperature, throw it out. Smell and taste cannot confirm the absence of foodborne pathogens.
