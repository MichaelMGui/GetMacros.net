Thyroid nutrition

Hyperthyroidism Nutrition, Weight and Bone Health

Hyperthyroidism accelerates metabolism and can cause weight loss, muscle loss, heat intolerance, diarrhea, rapid heartbeat and bone loss. Nutrition supports recovery but does not control hormone production.

Emergency signs: Fever, severe agitation, confusion, vomiting, chest pain or a very rapid heartbeat with known or suspected hyperthyroidism can signal thyroid storm and needs emergency care.

Protect energy and protein

Unintended loss may require frequent meals and protein-rich snacks. Eggs, yogurt, tofu, beans, fish, poultry and other tolerated foods can help preserve muscle while treatment takes effect.

Do not chase weight loss

Weight may return as thyroid levels normalize. Severe restriction during active disease can compound muscle and bone loss. Discuss body changes without using thyroid medicine as a weight-control tool.

Calcium and vitamin D matter

Excess thyroid hormone increases bone turnover. Use calcium-rich foods and individualized vitamin D guidance, alongside treatment and weight-bearing or resistance activity when medically safe.

Avoid high-dose iodine experiments

Iodine can trigger or worsen some hyperthyroid states. Kelp and iodine-containing products require review, and a low-iodine diet is used only for specific radioactive-iodine preparation—not routine care.

Caffeine may amplify symptoms

Coffee, energy drinks and pre-workouts can worsen tremor, palpitations or anxiety for some. Reduce gradually and review stimulants with the clinician.

Medication side effects need action

Antithyroid medicines can rarely affect white blood cells or liver function. Follow the prescriber’s warning instructions for fever, sore throat, jaundice or dark urine rather than trying to treat them with food.

Rebuild gradually

Once hormone levels improve, reassess energy needs and activity. Recovery includes muscle strength, sleep, bowel function and bone health—not only scale weight.
