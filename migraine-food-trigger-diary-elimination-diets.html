Migraine Food Triggers, Diaries and Elimination Diets

A diary can reveal patterns, but correlation is easy to overread when migraine attacks have many overlapping influences.

Do not self-test emergencies: New neurological symptoms, abrupt severe headache or a major change in pattern needs medical assessment, not a food challenge.

Record the whole context

Note headache onset, aura or other symptoms, severity, sleep, cycle timing, stress, weather, meals, fluids, caffeine, alcohol and acute medicine. Recording only food makes food look more important than it may be.

Watch for the premonitory phase

Yawning, fatigue, mood change, thirst and food cravings can begin before pain. A chocolate craving followed by migraine does not automatically mean chocolate caused it.

Change one variable

If a food is repeatedly suspicious, discuss a time-limited removal and planned reintroduction. Eliminating ten foods at once increases nutritional and social cost while weakening the experiment.

Use repeatability, not one event

Look for a consistent timing relationship across multiple exposures. Also compare days when the food is eaten without an attack and attacks that happen without the food.

Protect against restriction

Seek dietitian support if the safe-food list keeps shrinking, meals provoke anxiety, weight drops unintentionally or eating becomes secretive or compulsive.
