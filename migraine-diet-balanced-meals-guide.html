Migraine nutrition

Migraine Diet and Balanced Meals

There is no single migraine diet. Consistent, adequate nourishment may lower susceptibility for some people, but food does not explain every attack or replace preventive and acute treatment.

Emergency boundary: A sudden “worst headache,” headache with new weakness, confusion, seizure, fever and stiff neck, head injury, or new symptoms in pregnancy requires urgent assessment.

Start with consistency

Long gaps between meals can be a trigger for some people. Use a rhythm that fits your day: meals with protein, carbohydrate, produce and fat, plus snacks when needed. A rigid five-meal rule is not required.

There are no universal forbidden foods

Chocolate, cheese, MSG, citrus, processed meat and many other foods appear on online lists, but individual responses vary and some cravings may be an early migraine symptom rather than a cause.

A balanced baseline makes patterns clearer

Regular sleep, meals, fluids, movement and medicine use create a steadier context for a diary. If everything changes at once, it becomes impossible to know what mattered.

Avoid under-fueling

Aggressive calorie restriction, fasting or very-low-carbohydrate plans can create hunger, sleep disruption or dehydration. Weight goals should not sacrifice migraine care or trigger disordered eating.

Food is one part of treatment

Discuss frequent, disabling or changing headaches with a clinician. Acute and preventive medicines, devices, behavioural strategies and management of other conditions may all matter.
