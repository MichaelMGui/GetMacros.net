Levothyroxine Timing: Food, Coffee, Calcium and Iron

Levothyroxine works best when taken consistently. The goal is not a complicated food ban; it is a schedule that produces reliable absorption and reliable laboratory interpretation.

Never adjust the dose yourself. Too little and too much thyroid hormone can both cause harm. Pregnancy, major weight change, a brand change or new medicines may require faster review.

Follow the prescribed empty-stomach plan

Many people are instructed to take levothyroxine with water 30–60 minutes before breakfast. A bedtime schedule may work for some when separated from food, but use the prescriber’s directions.

Coffee can reduce absorption

Espresso and coffee taken close to tablets may reduce absorption. Rather than quitting coffee, separate it according to professional guidance and keep the interval consistent.

Calcium and iron commonly interfere

Calcium supplements, iron tablets, prenatal vitamins and some antacids can bind or reduce absorption. Ask a pharmacist for the required spacing because products and circumstances differ.

High-fibre and soy changes need consistency

Fibre and soy foods can be part of a nutritious diet. A major change in intake may alter medication needs, so keep the pattern stable and mention changes before testing.

Check every new product

Cholesterol medicines, acid-reducing medicines and other prescriptions can matter. Bring a complete medication and supplement list rather than relying on memory.

Lab timing matters after changes

TSH responds slowly. Clinicians commonly recheck several weeks after starting or changing dose. Testing too soon or changing the schedule repeatedly can obscure the picture.

Missed doses require personal instructions

Do not improvise repeated double doses. Follow the label and contact the prescriber when more than one dose is missed or when vomiting, surgery or inability to swallow disrupts the routine.
