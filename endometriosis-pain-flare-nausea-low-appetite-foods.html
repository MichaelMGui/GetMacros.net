Endometriosis nutrition

Endometriosis Pain Flares, Nausea and Low Appetite

Pain, nausea, fatigue and bowel symptoms can make cooking and eating hard. A flare-day plan should reduce effort and protect intake while medical treatment addresses the condition.

Do not assume a flare: Sudden severe pain, fever, repeated vomiting, fainting, a rigid swollen abdomen or possible pregnancy needs urgent assessment.

Lower the effort threshold

Keep foods that require little preparation: soup, yogurt or fortified alternatives, crackers, eggs, frozen rice, nut butter, canned beans or fish, smoothies and prepared meals. Eating something workable is better than waiting for a perfect anti-inflammatory meal.

Use smaller, more frequent opportunities

If nausea or fullness limits intake, try modest meals or snacks every few hours. Cool or room-temperature foods may smell less intense. Sip fluid regularly; oral rehydration products may be appropriate after significant vomiting or diarrhea, but are not everyday necessities.

Choose texture to match symptoms

During bowel discomfort, softer starches, cooked vegetables, soups and smooth proteins may feel easier. This is symptom accommodation, not proof those foods treat endometriosis. Return variety as tolerated.

Plan around pain medicine safely

Some anti-inflammatory medicines are taken with food to reduce stomach irritation; follow the label, prescriber or pharmacist. Black stool, vomiting blood or severe stomach pain requires urgent care.

Protect nutrition over time

Repeated low-intake days can lead to unintentional weight loss and nutrient gaps. Ask for dietitian support if eating is regularly disrupted, and tell the care team when symptoms prevent work, sleep, movement or meals.
