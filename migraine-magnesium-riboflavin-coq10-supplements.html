Migraine Supplements: Magnesium, Riboflavin and CoQ10

Magnesium, riboflavin and CoQ10 are used for migraine prevention, but they are treatment decisions with delayed outcomes—not instant nutrition fixes.

Professional guidance matters: Migraine-prevention magnesium doses can exceed the ordinary supplement upper limit. Kidney disease, pregnancy and medicine interactions require individual review.

Magnesium has the clearest common role

Small trials suggest modest preventive benefit, and professional guidance considers it probably effective. Diarrhea and cramping are common; forms and elemental amounts differ.

Riboflavin and CoQ10 are not interchangeable

Evidence, dose, cost and side effects differ. Riboflavin can turn urine bright yellow. CoQ10 can interact with some medicines. A combination product makes it harder to identify benefit or harm.

Avoid butterbur

Butterbur is no longer recommended because of liver-toxicity concerns and product contamination. “PA-free” marketing does not turn uncertain quality into a first-choice plan.

Prevention takes time

Agree on a trial length and track monthly migraine days, severity, disability and acute medicine use. Stop rules should cover intolerable gastrointestinal effects, rash, jaundice or other concerning symptoms.

Quality testing is not efficacy

Independent certification can support ingredient identity and contamination testing; it does not prove that the product prevents migraine.
