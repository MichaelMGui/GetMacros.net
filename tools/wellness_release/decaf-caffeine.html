Decaf coffee can still contain caffeine. The FDA gives a typical range of 2 to 15 mg per 8 U.S. fluid ounces. Coffee-shop studies and product listings may describe a different cup size, so read the unit before comparing the numbers.

Put sizes on the same basis

For the same brewed batch, 12 ounces at the same strength contains one and a half times as much caffeine as 8 ounces. So an 8-ounce cup with 6 mg becomes 9 mg at 12 ounces. If the larger drink uses a different brew or the recipe calls for two shots, that scaling may be wrong. An espresso-based drink is better checked by shot count and the cafe’s own ingredients than by dividing the total fluid ounces.

Read a range as a range

The study sampled particular shops in 2006. Its observed range describes those samples. A reported zero for one of those cups does not establish an expectation for every decaf cup or for the same menu name today. For one order, the current retailer or manufacturer is a better source than a historical sample or a generic estimate.

On U.S. packaged products, the FDA distinguishes caffeine added as its own ingredient from caffeine naturally present in ingredients like coffee or chocolate.
