Breakage is a fracture in a hair shaft. Shedding involves hairs released from the scalp. Some research looks at the shaft itself, while other studies count hairs shed during a standardized observation. A report that says only “fewer hairs” leaves the reader with a basic question: fewer fragments, or fewer hairs shed?

Follow the study’s measurement

One breakage paper used microscopy and X-ray tomography to examine damage in curly hair fibers. Another study standardized a short count to assess shedding in a sample of men without alopecia. Their outcomes are different even if a product post summarizes both as “hair loss research.”

Read a nutrition claim at the same level

Suppose a post credits a food with “stronger hair.” To assess the claim, find what was measured, whether a matched comparison existed and whether participants consumed that actual food. A shaft measurement can answer a structural question. A clinical assessment is needed to establish the cause of shedding in a particular person.
