A 2021 paper described 2,029 adults who answered an online survey after reporting they had followed a carnivore diet for at least six months. The participants described their food habits and perceived health, often favorably. The study provides a picture of those respondents. It does not measure what would happen to everyone who starts eating this way.

The six-month rule matters

Imagine, purely as an example, ten people try a diet and four stop in the first month. A survey that requires six months of adherence would miss those four. That does not tell us why they stopped. It shows why a sample of people who continued cannot measure the experiences of all starters. The authors identify this kind of selection bias as a limit of the survey.

Frequency leaves the portion open

The survey did not measure portion sizes. A report of eating an item daily gives no amount per day and no complete estimate of nutrients. It also does not establish which foods caused any reported change. If two people use the same diet name while eating different foods, compare their actual portions before interpreting a nutrition claim.
