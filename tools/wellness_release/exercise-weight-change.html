A scale gives a net change. Sports researchers estimating sweat loss also account for fluid drunk and urine passed during the measurement. A person can replace part of the sweat loss with drinks during exercise, so the scale alone can show a smaller difference.

MeasurementIllustrative valueHow it enters the estimate
Body mass before and after70.0 kg and 69.6 kgStart with 0.4 kg net loss.
Fluid consumed0.5 LAdd it back because the scale already includes the drink.
Urine passed0.1 LSubtract it because that part of the loss was via urine.

The simplified calculation is 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.1, or about 0.8 L of sweat for this example. Over one hour, the numerical rate is about 0.8 L per hour. This approximation uses the close relationship between kilograms of lost water and liters of fluid under controlled measurements.

When interpreting a study, note the scale precision, wet clothing and whether drinks and bathroom breaks were recorded. The result applies to the tested conditions and duration.
