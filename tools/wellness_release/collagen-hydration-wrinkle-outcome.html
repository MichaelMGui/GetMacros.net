A positive reading on skin moisture does not show that a product prevents wrinkles. Moisture, evaporative water loss and visible lines are different outcomes. To evaluate the claim on the package, find the outcome actually reported by the study it cites.

Follow the outcome through a real paper

A 2025 trial of 77 women compared a specific collagen peptide product with placebo for 12 weeks and measured several outcomes again four weeks later. It reported differences in moisture and water loss. The extra four weeks help interpret those measured outcomes. They do not extend the result to prevention of wrinkles. Three authors declared employment with a biotechnology company.

A separate 2025 pooled analysis examined outcomes including wrinkles. Its overall results and the analysis restricted to higher-quality trials differed. That makes the specific outcome and trial selection worth checking, rather than treating any positive skin result as interchangeable. Read more about the instruments behind hydration claims.
