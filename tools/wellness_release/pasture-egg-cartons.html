A pasture-raised claim by itself gives no milligram amount of omega-3 per egg. A controlled feeding and housing comparison can show that eggs from the studied hens differed. To find the amount in a specific product, look for a stated quantity and the serving it describes.

Four pieces of information to read separately
What you findWhat you can compare
A housing or pasture claimThe practice as described under that producer’s or certifier’s standard
An omega-3 amount per eggThe manufacturer’s stated amount for the named serving
An amount of DHA per eggOne named omega-3 fatty acid, rather than the total for all types
A relative study findingThe difference between the groups the researchers actually tested

What the pasture study compared

Karsten and colleagues compared eggs from hens with access to pasture and supplemental commercial feed with eggs from related hens eating commercial feed in cages. In their experiment the pastured group’s eggs had 2.5 times the total omega-3 fats. That ratio is specific to those groups. The same study found a higher concentration of vitamin A, yet total vitamin A per egg showed no difference. It is worth checking whether a finding applies to a concentration or the whole food.

Shop by the measurement you want

Suppose carton A lists 160 mg of total omega-3 per two eggs. That is 80 mg per egg. Suppose carton B lists 70 mg of DHA per egg. You can compare both on a per-egg basis, but you still cannot rank the two cartons for DHA because carton A gives a total and does not identify the amount of DHA. The carton numbers are illustrative.
