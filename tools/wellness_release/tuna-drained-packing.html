Draining canned tuna puts the two samples on a comparable serving basis. It does not make them nutritionally identical. In two USDA reference records for no-added-salt light tuna, the oil-packed drained solids list about 70 more kcal and 6.3 g more fat per 85 g than the water-packed drained solids.

Scale from the same weight

Both records provide an 85 g portion. Multiply the 100 g numbers by 0.85, then round for display. For example, 8.21 g of fat becomes about 7.0 g. The energy difference is about 70 kcal before rounding the two totals separately.

What the two records cannot isolate

These are distinct USDA reference entries. They are not measurements of the same batch of fish split between two packing liquids. They illustrate why a generic drained tuna entry can differ from what you ate. Exact results can vary with the fish, product and draining. If you pour oil from the can into a dressing, the drained solids record alone does not cover that separately used oil. If the label already covers the entire contents consumed, adding the pack liquid separately would double count it.

Use the actual drained weight and the matching entry for your diary. Package net weight may include liquid.
