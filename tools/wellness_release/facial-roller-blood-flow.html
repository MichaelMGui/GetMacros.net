A facial-roller study measured blood flow in cheek skin and how local vessels responded to heat. An increase shortly after using the roller and a different response after several weeks are separate results. Neither describes changes to facial bone shape.

Two experiments in the same facial roller paper
ExperimentWhere and whenReported finding
Short session, 12 participantsRight cheek, shortly after the sessionIncreased skin blood flow on the treated side for at least 10 minutes
Five-week intervention, 14 participantsRight cheek, in response to a controlled warming stimulusGreater vessel dilation in the treated cheek during the test

Look for the measurement behind ‘better circulation’

In this paper, blood flow shortly after contact was one outcome. How vessels reacted to a local heat test after the longer intervention was another. The longer study should not be read as proof that resting blood flow stayed higher for five weeks. Nor does a single treated cheek measurement give the response for the rest of the face.

The samples were small, and a co-author was affiliated with a company’s research centre. The report does not establish a general effect on appearance. For results on a different endpoint, see what a trial of facial contour measured.
