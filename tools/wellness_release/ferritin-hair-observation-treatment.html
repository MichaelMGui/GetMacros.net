A ferritin difference between groups can show that a measured iron-storage marker and the studied hair condition were associated. It cannot show that everyone with shedding would improve by taking iron. The diffuse-hair-loss studies here measured or compared lab values. They did not randomly assign the treatment in that claim and track the outcome.

Check who was included and what changed

When a post cites ferritin, check whether the study recruited people with one specified shedding pattern or mixed several conditions. Then ask whether the paper measured a baseline marker or measured visible change after an intervention. A change in the marker would still be a separate outcome from a change in hair.

A research cutoff is not a personal diagnosis. Clinical interpretation depends on the symptoms and circumstances of the person tested. See more examples of checking what a study actually measured.
