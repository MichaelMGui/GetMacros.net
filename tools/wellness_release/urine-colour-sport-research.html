Urine color can give a rough cue about how concentrated a sample is. Research on that cue typically checks color against other measurements in the same sample. A shade in one photograph cannot tell you what changed across an entire day.

What the athlete study actually measured

The study collected 803 samples from 474 elite athletes, including morning and other samples during rehydration. Researchers compared color readings from a spectrophotometer with urine osmolality and specific gravity. That tests whether an instrument color measurement can track urine concentration in that sample of athletes. It is different from a phone photograph or a chart seen under different lighting.

When comparing findings, check when the sample was taken and whether the study reported urine concentration or longer-term fluid outcomes. For a different short-term endpoint, see why the four-hour urine collection matters in beverage studies.
