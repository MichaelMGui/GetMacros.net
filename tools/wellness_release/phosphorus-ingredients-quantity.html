Phosphorus is a mineral that may be present even when the label omits its amount. The NIH notes that U.S. food labels generally do not have to list naturally occurring phosphorus. An ingredient such as phosphoric acid can identify a phosphorus-containing ingredient without stating the number of milligrams of phosphorus.

Keep the quantity field separate from the ingredient field

Half a cup of the listed milk scales to 120 mg phosphorus. There is no equivalent calculation for the named cola from the ingredient list alone. When you need a precise nutrient quantity for a brand, use its actual label or ask the manufacturer.

Apply the same approach to other nutrients that may be missing from a panel. The Nutrition Facts guide shows how to match a declared value to its portion.
