In the U.S., a dietary supplement structure/function claim describes an intended effect on normal body structure or function. It is different from the nutrient amount on the Supplement Facts panel and from a claim to treat a disease. The wording gives you a question to check against the evidence for the exact product.

Three fictional statements and the questions they raise
Label wordingWhat to check
“Magnesium, 100 mg per serving”The declared amount and the number of units in the serving
“Supports normal muscle function”The technical evidence behind the stated function
“Treats chronic insomnia”A disease-treatment claim, which raises separate regulatory and evidence requirements

What a notification means

FDA describes a process for manufacturers to notify the agency within 30 days after first marketing a supplement with certain claims. It also states that manufacturers need substantiation that a claim is truthful and not misleading. A notification is separate from FDA preapproval. The familiar disclaimer on affected labels is not independent evidence that a product works.

Match the outcome to the language

If a product cites a study about absorption, check whether the study also measured the promised function. Researchers might measure how much of a nutrient appears in urine without examining muscle performance. For an example of that distinction, see what a magnesium absorption trial measured.
