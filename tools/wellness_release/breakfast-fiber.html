Why the largest number can be a misleading shortcut

Two rows in this sample report 6 g fiber: Burger King’s Large Hash Browns and Chick-fil-A’s 10-count Chick-n-Minis. Those figures describe different orders, a large side and ten pieces. Chick-fil-A’s meatless Hash Brown Scramble Burrito lists 5 g per source burrito. If you order one burrito, that’s the portion to compare. Dividing the 10-count serving into two equal halves would estimate 3 g per half using the printed figure, without establishing what a different marketed size contains.

What the counts cover

We selected rows where the registry market is US and the meal tag is breakfast. Each source row counts once, so sizes and recipe variants remain separate. A numeric zero counts as reported, while a blank would stay in the total row count and out of the known-value column. The 3 g cutoff is used to illustrate the distribution. It is not a dietary recommendation.

The number of tracked rows varies substantially by chain. A chain with one entry has fewer chances to appear in the last column than one with fifty. Use the restaurant sources for individual portions. The counts describe the snapshot and do not measure the range of choices at every U.S. location or what is currently on sale.
