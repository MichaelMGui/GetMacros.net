For vitamin D, 1 microgram (mcg or µg) equals 40 International Units (IU). When a label prints both values beside the same serving, they describe the same amount. Add only after converting the figures to one unit and choosing the servings you mean to compare.

Then check the serving

Imagine one bottle declares 600 IU per two pieces and another declares 10 mcg per piece. One piece from the first has 300 IU, or 7.5 mcg. One piece from the second has 400 IU, or 10 mcg. The second has the larger amount per piece, while the first has the larger amount per labeled serving. These are example labels.

Keep percent Daily Value separate

Percent Daily Value describes a proportion of a labeling reference. It is neither another quantity to add nor the conversion factor between mcg and IU. For vitamin D in food, the NIH labeling explanation notes that the U.S. Nutrition Facts label uses mcg and may also print IU in parentheses.
