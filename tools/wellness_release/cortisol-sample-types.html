A blood or saliva sample usually captures cortisol near the time it was taken. A hair sample can provide information about exposure over a longer period as hair grows. Those measurements answer different questions and cannot be converted into one another by changing units.

One claim, two different experiments

Suppose a paper reports a change between saliva collected before an event and after it. The comparison describes two collection windows and whatever else happened between them. A second paper might compare hair segments between groups. The second measurement cannot tell you the height or timing of a short response to the first event. Likewise, one saliva measurement cannot stand in for months of exposure.

The design determines the conclusion

Manenschijn and colleagues examined whether cortisol measured in scalp hair reflected longer exposure under their study conditions. A method study by Meyer and colleagues likewise distinguished point samples from cortisol incorporated in hair. The time scale does not by itself identify the cause of the exposure. Check the specimen, sampling schedule and study design before repeating a headline about “high stress.” For a within-morning comparison, see the cortisol awakening guide.
