A sponsorship disclosure tells you about a relationship that could affect how you weigh a recommendation. It cannot tell you whether the product changed someone’s skin or hair. When a social post mixes a story about someone’s routine with an effectiveness claim, read the disclosure and the evidence separately.

Put the claim in one sentence

If a product post says it made hair “look thicker,” ask whether that means hair appeared fuller in a styled photo or someone measured more hairs per unit area. The evidence needed for the second claim is different. In an ingredient study, check whether that exact product was tested and whether the authors measured the same outcome as the caption.

Why placement matters

The U.S. FTC’s guidance explains that material connections can include payment, gifts or discounts. It also recommends placing a disclosure where people will see it with the endorsement. A free product can be worth disclosing even if no brand script was supplied. If you cannot tell who provided the product or which item the relationship covers, that uncertainty stays separate from the merits of the claim.
