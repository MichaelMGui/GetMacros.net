Morning cortisol can already be rising before you wake. A measurement taken at waking and another 50 minutes later can show a further rise, but the two points do not reveal when it began or whether waking sped it up. Researchers need different sampling designs to answer those questions.

Two study designs for separating body-clock time and waking
Study approachQuestion it can address
Laboratory sleep and wake times scheduled across the biological clockDoes the difference between waking and a later saliva sample vary with internal time?
Sampling that spans the period before and after wakingDoes the estimated rate of increase become steeper immediately after waking?

Look for when the first measurement happens

The laboratory study found that the size of the post-waking difference varied with circadian phase. The study using collection across the wake boundary did not find a steeper cortisol increase after waking than before. Those findings can coexist. An upward trend visible after waking does not by itself identify a change in slope at waking.

When a morning routine claims to change the response, check whether researchers identified actual wake time, sampled more than once and described which outcome they measured. See ways to read outcomes behind wellness claims.
