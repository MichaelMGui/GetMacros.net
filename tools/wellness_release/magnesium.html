In these selected USDA reference foods, a 28 g portion of roasted pumpkin seed kernels supplies about 154 mg of magnesium. Boiled black beans and drained boiled spinach also contribute. Which food supplies more on your plate depends on how much you eat and how it was prepared.

Compare the topping you use

A 10 g sprinkle of the pumpkin seeds contributes about 55 mg. That is slightly less than the 60 mg in half a cup of the beans. The concentrated seed kernels lead by weight, while the bigger portion of beans can lead on the plate. In a bean bowl with both, the two components contribute about 115 mg before other ingredients.

Match the preparation

Use dry or roasted seed kernels only when that matches the food you weighed. The spinach reference describes cooked, drained leaves. A cup of raw spinach occupies the same volume as a cup of cooked spinach but can contain a different weight of leaves. Use the corresponding raw entry if that is what you ate.

Check the reference and label separately

NIH lists selected magnesium sources in its own common portions, including roasted pumpkin seeds and cooked black beans. Small differences between tables can reflect different portion weights or rounding. A packaged product’s nutrition panel can omit magnesium even if the food supplies some. A blank field in a database is also unknown. Use the catalogue to compare stated amounts and keep the source beside the number.
