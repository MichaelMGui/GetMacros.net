One teaspoon of the sunflower oil entry in the NIH food table supplies about 1.87 mg of alpha-tocopherol. That is a third of the listed 5.6 mg tablespoon. The same teaspoon measure works out to 1.53 mg for the safflower oil entry and 6.77 mg for wheat germ oil. These are estimates using the table’s named oils.

Split the oil across the food served

If a dressing has two tablespoons of sunflower oil and four people eat equal shares of all the dressing, the oil contributes about 2.8 mg each. Count half a tablespoon per person. If a quarter of the dressing stays in the bowl and the rest is shared equally, the estimate falls to 2.1 mg per person.

These examples count only the oil’s contribution. Ingredients such as seeds can bring their own vitamin E, and a named grocery oil may differ from a generic reference. Keep the calculation unrounded until the last step if you add up several small amounts. Compare vitamin E from nuts and seeds.
