Muscles can measure larger immediately after a session. In an ultrasound study of 22 untrained young men, researchers recorded an average 8.3% increase in their combined thigh muscle thickness measure just after the first session and a 2.9% increase after six weeks of training. Those measurements refer to different times and cannot be read as the same type of change.

Ask when the photo was taken

A post-workout image paired with a rested starting image gives two different measurement conditions. For a photo comparison, record the time since exercise, posture, camera position and whether the muscle was flexed. That makes the photos more comparable. The image still cannot distinguish transient swelling from long-term tissue change on its own.

The percentages above describe a particular ultrasound protocol. They do not tell you how much an individual ought to change or how photos would look at those time points. See how camera distance changes a photo comparison for a separate visual effect.
