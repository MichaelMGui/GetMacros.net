Start with the serving size on the U.S. Supplement Facts panel. An amount listed for two capsules belongs to those two capsules together. The amount on the front of the bottle may refer to the serving, a blend, or the bottle’s marketing description. The panel tells you which amount is actually declared.

Label A shows a bigger amount per stated serving. Label B shows a bigger amount per capsule. Those are different comparisons. The example illustrates label arithmetic, with no suggested amount to take. Some products combine different capsules or packets in one serving. If the label does not allocate ingredients among them, do not divide the amount across the units and present the result as an exact figure.

A blend total belongs to the whole blend

If a panel reports 500 mg for three ingredients in a proprietary blend, 500 mg refers to the combined blend. You cannot infer that each ingredient contributes 500 mg, or that each supplies one third. Choose exact ingredient amounts for comparisons only when the label declares them.

Keep an amount separate from an approval claim

FDA guidance says dietary supplements generally reach the U.S. market without FDA approval for safety or effectiveness beforehand. A Supplement Facts panel declares what the company lists. Examine evidence for a product claim separately. If a label amount matters for a conversation with your pharmacist or clinician, bring the panel, the serving size and the amount you actually use.
