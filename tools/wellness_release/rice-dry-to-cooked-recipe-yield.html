One dry cup yields about 3⅝ cooked cups in the USDA example for long-grain parboiled brown rice. Its listing for enriched, parboiled long-grain white rice gives about 2¾ cooked cups. The product descriptions matter. These are specific approximate yields, so use the weight or volume of your finished batch for a portion you actually served.

Use the dry label when building the recipe and the cooked yield when serving

Suppose you record the dry rice in a recipe, cook it in plain water and weigh the finished batch at 500 g. A 125 g serving is one-quarter of the finished yield, so assign it one-quarter of the recorded ingredients as a practical estimate. If you use broth or oil, enter those separately. Added water changes weight and volume.

Record either a share of the batch or the appropriate cooked-food reference for the serving. Recording both counts the rice twice. Compare cooked rice by cups and grams.
