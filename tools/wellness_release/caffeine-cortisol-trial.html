A controlled caffeine study found that several days of regular exposure reduced the cortisol response to the first morning caffeine dose, while a response appeared again later that day. The result depended on when researchers sampled and which prior intake they assigned.

Five days with assigned daily caffeineStudy did not detect the initial cortisol response seen after abstinenceElevation was detectable after the early-afternoon dose

The headline often leaves out the control and the time of day

The 96 participants took caffeine or placebo capsules on specified schedules in a crossover design. Researchers sampled saliva from morning into evening. Cortisol later returned toward the control level. This protocol separates a prior exposure pattern from an isolated challenge, so a result at one test time cannot answer for the entire day.

The study measured salivary cortisol. It did not test whether an ordinary coffee makes someone feel more stressed or whether holding off on coffee changes long-term health. For the separate question of timing and sleep, see the caffeine and bedtime trials.
