Fish oil is the whole oil mixture. EPA and DHA are two fatty acids that may be listed within it. In an archived ALLMAX Essentials Omega 3 label held by the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database, one softgel declares 1,000 mg of fish oil concentrate, including 180 mg of EPA and 120 mg of DHA. Those two lines add to 300 mg.

Read one archived fish-oil label from the largest amount to its parts
Printed line or calculationAmount per listed softgel
Fish oil concentrate1,000 mg

DHA120 mg
EPA plus DHA, calculated300 mg

Notice what is included in the larger line

In this example the two named fatty acids account for 30% of the declared concentrate by mass. Adding all three lines would count the EPA and DHA twice. The remaining 700 mg is simply the amount of the concentrate outside those two declared components, based on the printed figures. The label does not specify its full composition.

Compare the same amount of product

If another label uses a serving of two softgels, divide its EPA and DHA amounts by two to compare one softgel. Use the current package if it differs from the archive. The label database preserves a statement from the manufacturer, not a lab test of an individual bottle. See the Supplement Facts serving-size guide for a separate per-capsule example.
