Evidence for creatine monohydrate describes the form tested in that study. If a blend uses different forms or combines creatine with other ingredients, look for evidence about that formulation. A shared ingredient name does not establish that the entire blend produces the same effect.

Match the form, comparator and outcome

Suppose a blend ad cites the first paper to say an added ingredient improves endurance. Check what the study groups received and whether endurance was the outcome. A resistance training result for one creatine form would be insufficient on both points. Likewise, a difference in blood markers cannot be treated as a result for every sport or activity.

Read “no advantage” at the study’s scale

A study can fail to show an advantage because the formulations performed similarly under the tested conditions or because the sample cannot resolve a smaller difference. The wording does not establish that all products are equivalent. If the evidence refers to hair rather than training, read the separate hair research comparison to see which outcome those studies measured.
