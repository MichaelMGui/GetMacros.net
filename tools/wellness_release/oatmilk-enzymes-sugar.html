An oat milk can show added sugar on its U.S. Nutrition Facts panel even when cane sugar is absent from the ingredient list. Enzymes can convert some of the oats’ starch to sugars such as maltose. FDA guidance treats sugars made through controlled breakdown of starch in products such as oat beverages as added sugars.

Oatly attributes the Full Fat drink’s listed sugar to its oat-processing step. Its Unsweetened recipe uses a process designed to leave less maltose. If your coffee contains the equivalent of a quarter cup of the Full Fat drink, the label works out to about 1.75 g total sugar, including about 1.75 g added sugar. The two equal numbers refer to the same grams.

The Unsweetened example still lists 6 g total carbohydrate in a full cup. Carbohydrate includes more than sugars. For another variant or country, check the local package and the amount you poured. Use the Nutrition Facts guide to match the serving first.
