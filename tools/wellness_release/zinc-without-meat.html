Roasted pumpkin seeds, lentils, cheddar and plain Greek yogurt all supply zinc. The best comparison starts with the portion you would put on the plate. The NIH table gives an ounce of pumpkin seeds 2.2 mg of zinc and half a cup of cooked lentils 1.3 mg. A half-ounce topping brings the seeds to about 1.1 mg and reverses that comparison.

A small topping and a bowl of lentils

Half a cup of lentils with half an ounce of pumpkin seeds comes to about 2.4 mg of zinc. Use the weight of the kernels you actually eat. A bag’s serving may refer to an ounce of seeds, while the small handful you add to lunch may be less. The half-ounce value in the table uses the NIH value divided by two, so it inherits the rounding of the original entry.

What the food table can tell you

These numbers measure zinc content. The body absorbs some of that zinc. NIH notes that phytate in beans, seeds and whole grains can bind zinc and limit absorption. A bowl with 2.4 mg in its ingredients therefore gives a food-content estimate. It cannot tell you the amount your body absorbed or establish whether your overall pattern meets your needs.

When comparing fortified cereals or another packaged alternative, look at the stated serving and use the exact product label. A calculation based on cereal plus milk also needs a separate milk amount unless the label explicitly reports the prepared bowl.
