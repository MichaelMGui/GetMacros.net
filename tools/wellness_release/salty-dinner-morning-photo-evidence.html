Sodium can affect fluid balance. That is different from identifying the cause of puffiness in one morning photo. Controlled sodium studies here measured water balance or vascular outcomes. They did not test whether a particular salty dinner made faces look puffier the next day.

What a next-morning appearance claim needs
Part of the claimUseful evidence
What was in dinnerA specified sodium dose or measured meal with a comparable control.
What changed overnightMeasurements made before and afterward, ideally separating the tested exposure from other changes.
What changed in the faceA defined facial outcome under controlled timing and capture conditions, beyond a general water measurement.

Keep the claim at the level the study tested

A sodium-balance experiment can help interpret what happened in a controlled intervention. It cannot verify a one-off change that happened after a different meal. A photo also changes with lighting, position and other conditions. Check those before making any inference from appearance, and avoid using a photo alone to set a dietary rule.
