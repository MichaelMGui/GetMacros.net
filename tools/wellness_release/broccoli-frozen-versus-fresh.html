In USDA reference data, cooked broccoli listed without salt provides 64.9 mg vitamin C per 100 g. The cooked frozen, chopped broccoli entry provides 40.1 mg in the same weight. Both entries describe broccoli that was boiled and drained. The numbers show the difference between those entries, while the effects of freezing alone require a controlled comparison.

Matching a scoop narrows the difference

The gap is 24.8 mg per 100 g and about 13.7 mg for the listed half-cups. The frozen preparation’s reference scoop weighs more, partially offsetting the difference per gram. If your package is steamed in the bag or you roast it from frozen, the table can only give context. Its portions are boiled and drained.

In one experiment using the Marathon variety, blanching accounted for much of the loss during the studied frozen-storage process. A separate industrial-processing study found that frozen broccoli retained vitamin C after its particular process. Processing, time and preparation all matter.
