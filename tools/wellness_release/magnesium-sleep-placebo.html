In a four-week magnesium bisglycinate trial, the average insomnia questionnaire score fell in both groups. The reported drop was 3.9 points for magnesium and 2.3 points for placebo. The simple difference in those reported average drops is about 1.6 points. The full 3.9-point change includes whatever also contributed to improvement over time in the comparison group.

Why a comparison group matters

Symptoms can fluctuate, and people may join a study when the problem is unusually bothersome. Participants can also change their routine or expectations after enrolling. A randomized placebo comparison helps account for changes that can occur without the tested ingredient. It cannot identify which specific factor accounts for every point of the placebo group’s change.

Ask what a point represents

The result is a change on the Insomnia Severity Index, a questionnaire. A change of 1.6 points cannot be translated directly into minutes asleep. The authors report the statistical comparison in the paper. To judge whether the size of a change matters, also look for uncertainty, clinically meaningful differences and replication. The article describes the trial’s findings and does not recommend a supplement.
