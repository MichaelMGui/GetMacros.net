If you eat the entire contents of a package and its U.S. Nutrition Facts label has a whole-package column, use that column. Multiplying the rounded figures in the serving column can give a slightly different result. If you ate part of the package, match an actual weight or a defined fraction.

Why a shortcut can give 375 instead of 380

Suppose the underlying value were 152 kcal per 30 g. Rounded for the serving label, it would show 150. The whole bag contains 2.5 times the portion and works out to 380 kcal from the underlying value. Multiplying the printed 150 by 2.5 gives 375. When both columns are available, the one that describes your full package avoids this second calculation.

For a partially eaten bag, the estimate assumes that your portion is representative of the contents. If the food has distinct components, such as chips and a dip, weigh or record them separately when possible. Log the amount you actually ate in the food diary.
