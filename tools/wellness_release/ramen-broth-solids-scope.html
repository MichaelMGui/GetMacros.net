A whole-bowl nutrition value describes a stated preparation. When you leave broth, the amount of salt and other nutrients you consumed can change. In a 2026 cafeteria crossover study, students consumed less soup and less estimated salt when using a perforated spoon. The size of that difference applies to the studied bowl and conditions.

Which figures can support a diary entry?
What you knowWhat you can useWhat remains unknown
You ate the whole listed bowl.The complete bowl entry if the preparation matches.Ordinary serving variation.
You used a measured stock in a home recipe.Its label and the amount retained in the portion.Nutrients transferred to the other ingredients during cooking.
You left half the broth of a prepared bowl with only a total label.A note about the fraction left and any separately documented ingredients.The exact sodium left in the soup and the amount in the solids.

Read the measured example

Among the study’s male participants, median soup intake was 141 g with a regular spoon and 95 g with the perforated spoon. The corresponding reported median salt intakes were 3.1 g and 2.4 g. That contrast does not imply a fixed amount of salt per gram left in any bowl. It also reports salt, a separate unit from sodium.

Use an exact separate stock quantity when the recipe and label support it. Otherwise keep the total marked as uncertain. See the salt and sodium conversion guide for the unit distinction.
