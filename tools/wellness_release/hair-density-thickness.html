Hair density tells you how many shafts are visible in a defined area. Shaft thickness is the width of an individual hair or the average width of sampled hairs. More hairs in an area and wider hairs are two different ways a study could describe an appearance effect.

Read the noun after “increased”

Compared with A, B has wider shafts with the same count per area. C has a higher count and the same average width. A headline about thicker hair could refer to either result or simply an impression in a photo. The table illustrates the units; its numbers are not normal ranges or diagnostic targets.

Match the scalp site and method

A trichoscopy study in healthy adult Egyptian women reported the number of hairs and shaft width separately, and examined different areas of the scalp. That design shows why sampling site and measurement units matter. Before using a nutrition study to support a claim about hair, look for the exact change measured and whether the comparison used the same site and method. A breakage or shedding count is a separate outcome again.
