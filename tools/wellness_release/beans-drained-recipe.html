The sodium in a recipe depends on whether the canned bean entry refers to beans with their liquid, drained solids or drained-and-rinsed beans. In a multi-brand study of canned beans, the averages reported for the source’s servings differed across the three preparation states.

Mean sodium per label-size serving across the samples in the study
Preparation testedReported mean sodium
Beans with packing liquid503 mg
Beans after draining321 mg
Beans after draining and rinsing295 mg

Make the recipe arithmetic match the entry

For an illustrative batch using two servings at the study’s drained mean, the beans contribute 642 mg of sodium. If that batch makes four equal portions, that is about 161 mg per portion from beans. Broth, salt and sauces still have separate amounts. Using 503 mg per source serving would give a different estimate because that entry includes packing liquid.

A study average does not identify every can

The study tested multiple brands and varieties. Its numbers illustrate why preparation matters rather than providing a fixed correction for every package. Use the product’s matching prepared entry when available. If the label does not say which basis it uses, retain that uncertainty in your diary.
