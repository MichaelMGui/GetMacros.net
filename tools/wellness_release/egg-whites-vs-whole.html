Four whites separated from large eggs weigh about 132 g in USDA reference data and give about 14.4 g protein. Two large whole eggs weigh about 100 g without shells and give 12.6 g protein. If you match the weight instead of the number of eggs, the whole-egg entry has more protein per 100 g.

Count ingredients the way you actually use them

Whites weigh around 33 g each in the reference. If your recipe takes one whole large egg and two whites, the estimates are 6.3 g plus about 7.2 g protein, for 13.5 g before additions. This is an ingredient calculation. Match your carton label if you pour packaged whites rather than separating eggs.

The whole egg also brings fat and nutrients from the yolk. Check the two-egg micronutrient comparison for a cooked portion. Milk or oil in an omelet needs its own entry.
