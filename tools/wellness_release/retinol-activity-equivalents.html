A value in micrograms of beta-carotene describes an amount of that compound. The vitamin A total in micrograms of retinol activity equivalents, or mcg RAE, describes estimated vitamin A activity. In the NIH reference system, 12 mcg of beta-carotene from food contributes 1 mcg RAE.

Look for a combined vitamin A line first

If a food record gives a vitamin A total of 100 mcg RAE and also gives 1,200 mcg beta-carotene, the carotenoid may already be included in that total. Use the combined value for a diary summary. Recalculate only when you have ingredient-level values and know which forms the source includes.

Old records may report international units (IU), for which the conversion depends on the form and whether the source is food or a supplement. The NIH table gives different factors. Keep the original form and unit beside the value when you compare records. See the Nutrient Density Explorer to compare vitamin A on the same footing across its reference foods.
