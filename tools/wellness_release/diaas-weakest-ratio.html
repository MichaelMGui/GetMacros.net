A digestible indispensable amino acid score (DIAAS) describes the weakest amino-acid ratio in a test protein against an age-specific reference pattern. It uses the digestible amounts of individual indispensable amino acids. A score of 80 therefore tells you something about the limiting amino acid, rather than a fraction to multiply by all the protein grams on a label.

Find the smallest ratio only after checking every amino acid

If all the other required amino acids also meet or exceed 0.80 in this example, the FAO equation gives 100 × 0.80, or 80. If another ratio is 0.65, that one limits the score and the result is 65. Neither answer lets you turn the amount shown on a food label into a single number for grams absorbed.

A complete calculation needs the amino-acid profile and the corresponding ileal digestibility data. It also needs the correct age-specific pattern. When you’re comparing a food’s portion, start with the grams you can actually verify. Compare common food portions by protein and fiber.
