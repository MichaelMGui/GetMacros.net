On a U.S. label, DFE means dietary folate equivalents. A package can list a total folate amount in mcg DFE and then give the folic acid amount in parentheses. The parenthetical amount is part of the total calculation. Adding it again would count the same folic acid twice.

Read the number and its unit together

One invented U.S. label and two ways to misread it
TextMeaning
“200 mcg DFE (120 mcg folic acid)”The hypothetical serving supplies 200 mcg dietary folate equivalents, represented in this example by 120 mcg folic acid.
“320 mcg total”This adds the parenthetical quantity to the total and mixes measurement conventions.
“200 mcg folic acid”This changes the unit. The label shown here declares 120 mcg folic acid.

Why the numbers differ in the first place

The NIH fact sheet explains that these equivalents account for differences in bioavailability. For folic acid in fortified foods, 0.6 mcg folic acid corresponds to 1 mcg DFE. Divide the 120 mcg in the example by 0.6 to get 200 mcg DFE. Naturally occurring food folate uses a one-to-one relationship. The conversion here specifically describes folic acid in food and should not be applied to every supplemental form or use condition.
