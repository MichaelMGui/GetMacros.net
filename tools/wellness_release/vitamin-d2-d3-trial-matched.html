Vitamin D2 and D3 can both contribute to vitamin D intake. In one UK winter trial, D3 led to a greater increase in total blood 25-hydroxyvitamin D than D2 when the researchers matched the vitamin amount and the kind of fortified food. The endpoint was a blood concentration.

Keep a label unit separate from a blood unit

The study randomized 335 healthy South Asian and white European women and ran for 12 weeks. The vitamin amounts in the foods are in micrograms. The blood test reports nanomoles per litre. The table compares the changes in the matched arms, rather than subtracting two nutrient label amounts or describing the size of a change for one individual.

In a smaller malted drink study with a different design, researchers found no significant difference between the forms at the amounts they tested. Trial design, participants, delivery and the endpoint affect how widely a result applies. The vitamin guides include food-label examples for a separate question about the amount in a serving.
