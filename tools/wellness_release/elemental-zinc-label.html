On a U.S. Supplement Facts panel, “Zinc 15 mg (as zinc gluconate)” gives the elemental zinc amount per labeled serving. Zinc gluconate names the source compound. The weight of that compound includes other atoms and is a different quantity from the declared zinc.

How to read a fictional two-lozenge label
What the panel saysWhat to record
Serving Size: 2 lozengesThe declared amounts refer to two units.
Zinc: 15 mg (as zinc gluconate)15 mg elemental zinc per two lozenges, or 7.5 mg per lozenge on average.
Source ingredient: zinc gluconateThe compound supplies the declared zinc. Keep the form as a separate note.

Compare the same thing across packages

First divide by the number of capsules or lozenges when the serving sizes differ. Use the named zinc line from each panel. If a listing gives only a mass of a zinc compound and no elemental amount, check the actual package before assuming equivalence. The Supplement Facts field guide shows where serving sizes appear.

This worksheet helps read labeled quantities. It does not determine a zinc intake to use. The NIH notes that excessive intake over time can affect copper status, which is one reason the same zinc should not be overlooked across multiple products.
