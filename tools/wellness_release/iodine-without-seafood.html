Eggs, dairy and iodized salt are among the foods listed in the NIH iodine table. The word iodized matters. At the same quarter-teaspoon measure, the table lists 78 mcg for iodized table salt and 0 mcg for the noniodized sea salt example.

Look past the bread’s name

Two slices of the listed whole-wheat bread made without iodate provide about 1 mcg. Potassium iodate and calcium iodate are examples of dough conditioners that can change the iodine content. An egg and an ounce of cheddar yield roughly 45 mcg on their own in this table. Count the bread only once you know you have the right reference or label.

The table entries are examples, and the salt comparison explains a labeling distinction. It does not call for increasing salt intake. Seaweed iodine can vary widely. Keep the exact food and preparation alongside any iodine figure used in the diary.
