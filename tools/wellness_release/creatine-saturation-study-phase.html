A loading phase tests a short, front-loaded part of a protocol. In an original study of 31 men, total muscle creatine rose about 20% after six days under one protocol. A more gradual protocol produced a similar reported rise after 28 days. The experiment’s endpoint was muscle creatine concentration, which is distinct from exercise performance or muscle size.

Different phases of the Hultman et al. experiment
Time point or phaseQuestion the measurement answered
First six daysHow quickly did concentration change under the short protocol?
Four weeksCould a more gradual protocol produce a similar change by a later date?
After the initial riseHow long did the observed change persist during maintenance or after supplement use stopped?

Make sure the comparison uses the same outcome

Two studies can agree about four-week concentration while giving different answers about first-week concentration. A longer trial measuring strength introduces another question entirely. Look for what happened in the same measurement window before comparing the studies’ conclusions.

The time points summarize these research protocols and the participants studied. They do not establish a schedule for an individual reader or a timeline for visible tissue change. See how a separate study interpreted weight and water.
