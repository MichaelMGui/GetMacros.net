A third-party quality seal can tell you about tests or manufacturing checks defined by its issuer. The USP Verified Mark, for example, represents a specific verification program for dietary supplements. A statement like “tested by a third party” gives fewer details until the manufacturer identifies who tested the product and what the test covered.

Three questions behind a quality seal
QuestionEvidence to look for
Does the program cover the ingredient amount?The issuer’s definition of the mark and its verification criteria
Does the exact product carry the mark?The exact name, formula and package matched against the issuer’s current program information
Does it improve the advertised outcome?A relevant study of the same product or formulation using the advertised outcome

Read the issuer’s scope

USP describes checks for listed ingredients and amounts, certain contaminants, manufacturing practices and how a supplement breaks down or releases ingredients under applicable test conditions. This information is about product quality. A quality check cannot replace a trial showing whether the product achieves a clinical or performance result.

Check a fictional product listing

Imagine a store shows a verified mark beside a product family with three formulations. Match the exact formula you are considering to the issuer’s listing. A mark on one formulation is not evidence for the other two. A screenshot of an old label can also be out of date. The Supplement Facts guide shows where to check ingredient and serving details once the exact product is identified.
