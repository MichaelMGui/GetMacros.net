In the NIH food table, three ounces of cooked yellowfin tuna list 92 micrograms of selenium. Three ounces of drained canned sardines list 45 micrograms, a large hard-boiled egg lists 15, and a cup of cooked oats lists 13. The portion makes as much difference to this comparison as the name of the food.

A small serving can change what comes first

An ounce of the reference sardines works out to about 15 mcg using the three-ounce figure. That is about the same as one reference egg. If a plate has both, the subtotal is about 30 mcg before anything else is counted. The equation is a comparison of food content, not a measurement of the actual brands or batch on the table.

Use the exact food description

The tuna row describes cooked yellowfin, while a can of light tuna can represent another species or preparation. NIH also notes that selenium in plant foods can vary with soils and source region. A single reference entry cannot establish the exact content of every bag of oats or any unlabeled food. For other nutrients in a named reference food, see the nutrient explorer.
