The PREDIMED study compared two supported Mediterranean eating interventions with advice to reduce dietary fat. It enrolled 7,447 adults in Spain at high cardiovascular risk, without cardiovascular disease at enrollment. The corrected 2018 report found fewer events in the two Mediterranean groups under its revised analyses.

The investigators withdrew the earlier paper after finding that some assignments did not follow the intended individual randomization. At one site, participants were assigned by clinic. The corrected report used analyses that did not rely entirely on every participant being individually randomized. The percentages in the table are observed group proportions, separate from the adjusted time-to-event estimates reported in the paper.

The intervention was broader than a single food

Both intervention groups received guidance about a pattern and the assigned foods. The study does not isolate what would happen if you add one product to an otherwise unchanged diet. It also does not estimate anyone’s personal risk. Check the population, comparator and measured endpoint when you see the trial cited for a claim about oil, nuts or a product described as Mediterranean.
