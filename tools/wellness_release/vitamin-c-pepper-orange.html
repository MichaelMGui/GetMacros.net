A half cup of raw sweet red pepper has about 95 mg vitamin C in the NIH table, compared with 70 mg for one medium orange. A smaller pepper portion can reverse that result. A quarter cup contributes about 48 mg using half the published amount.

Compare the way you eat the food

If you eat a medium orange and add a quarter cup of sliced red pepper to lunch, the two amounts add up to about 118 mg based on the source’s printed values. That is a sum for this example, separate from whatever else was on the plate. A cup of liquid and a cup of sliced food do not necessarily weigh the same, which is why a same-weight ranking is a different comparison.

Use the right preparation row

The pepper row describes raw pepper. Vitamin C is water soluble and can be lost during cooking and storage. A cooked vegetable may also weigh differently from the raw vegetable. If a table lists a different vitamin C amount per cup after cooking, first check the cup weight and preparation. The two cup values alone do not measure how much was lost from one piece of produce.
