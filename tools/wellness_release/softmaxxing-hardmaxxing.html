People who use “softmaxxing” generally mean appearance changes such as styling, grooming or skin care. “Hardmaxxing” usually refers to attempts to change physical features through procedures or other higher-risk interventions. These are informal labels. Two posts can use the same word for very different actions.

Terms tell you about the community using them

The American Academy of Dermatology describes a range from grooming to surgery and unregulated interventions. Its account illustrates why a product marketed as “soft” still needs a safety assessment on its own terms. In Ofcom’s qualitative UK research, some teenagers described encountering appearance ideals and extreme advice together. Those accounts show what can appear in a feed. They cannot tell you how widespread any particular practice is.

If a post jumps from a styling preference to a claim about anatomy, start with the evidence for the second claim. The before-and-after photo checklist explains how a photo setup alone can change what you see.
