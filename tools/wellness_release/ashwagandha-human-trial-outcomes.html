“Adaptogen” is a broad description in supplement marketing. The cited ashwagandha trials assigned specific extracts and measured defined outcomes, such as responses on stress or anxiety scales and hormone measurements. Those observations can be evaluated. A larger claim about adapting to every form of stress would require evidence that covers those situations.

Match a claim to the measured outcome
Reported resultWhat to verify in the study
Questionnaire scoreFind the scale, who completed it, and the difference versus placebo over the trial.
Cortisol resultCheck which specimen was measured, at what time and under what conditions.
Claim about a different productCompare plant part, extract and formulation before treating the results as equivalent.

Stay with the specific comparison

Two trials can both use the plant’s name while studying different extracts and starting populations. Even within the same trial, a questionnaire and a laboratory value need separate interpretation. Check whether the placebo comparison supports the advertised outcome and avoid replacing it with a before-and-after figure from one group.
