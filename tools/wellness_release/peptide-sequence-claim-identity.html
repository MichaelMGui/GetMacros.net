A peptide is a chain of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. The name of that category does not tell you which amino acids are present, their order, or what the product does. Some labels specify a named single peptide. Others describe a mixture, such as peptides from a hydrolyzed protein.

Three levels of detail behind a product description
DetailWhat it helps establish
Sequence or named identityWhich chain is being described, or whether the product is a mixture.
Final formulationWhether a product contains that substance alone, in a complex, or alongside other ingredients.
Research contextHow the product was studied and which outcome was measured.

Match the ingredient with the study before comparing products

If a paper studied one identified peptide, a label that only says “peptide blend” does not establish that the same substance was used. Even if a name matches, the delivery route and other formulation details can change what evidence applies. Check a product claim at the level of detail the cited source provides.
