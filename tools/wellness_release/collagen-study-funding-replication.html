A funding disclosure helps readers assess a result’s context. In a 2025 meta-analysis of 23 randomized collagen trials, the pooled analysis reported favorable skin outcomes. Subsets without pharmaceutical company funding did not show statistically clear improvements in the measured outcomes. The study groups can differ in ways besides funding, so the contrast raises a question for independent replication.

Three ways to read the same research area
MethodWhat it asksRemaining question
Pool the included trialsWhat is the average across studies the review selected?Were ingredients, measurements and participants similar enough?
Split by fundingDid the findings hold across funding categories?Were there also differences in study size or quality?
Examine risk of biasCould aspects of design or reporting affect confidence?Would a registered, independent study reproduce the same outcome?

A later systematic review counted trials with favorable results on particular measures and also assessed most included trials as having a high risk of bias. Counting favorable trials and pooling results and uncertainty are different approaches. Look for the outcome definition, the effect size and who decided what would be measured.

An independent repeat would use a comparable product, test the same endpoint, and publish its prespecified analysis. See GetMacros’ editorial policy for how sources and study limits are reported.
