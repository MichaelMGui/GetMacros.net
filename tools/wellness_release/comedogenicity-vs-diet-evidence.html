A noncomedogenic claim is about a product’s tendency to contribute to clogged pores under the conditions considered. It does not supply evidence about eating an ingredient with a similar name. Historical product testing has included different assay methods, so find out what exactly was tested before treating a label as a result for every person.

What evidence would address each claim
ClaimWhat to find in the source
“This formulation is noncomedogenic”The specific product, how it was applied, the test method and how clogged pores were assessed.
“Eating this food changes acne”The actual dietary exposure, comparison group, and how acne was measured over time.

Check the finished product and intended use

An ingredient result does not necessarily predict the finished formulation, and contact with a patch of skin does not reproduce eating the same ingredient. The FDA’s guidance also distinguishes ordinary cosmetic purposes from a claim that a product is intended to treat a condition. These are separate questions from whether a trial saw an effect.
