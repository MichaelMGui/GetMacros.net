In a U.S. study published in 2023, 3 of 25 tested melatonin gummies had a measured amount within 10% of the declared amount. The figure applies to the products and samples the researchers tested. It does not establish the accuracy of a package bought today.

How to read the reported measurements
Study resultMeaning
3 of 25 within 10% of the labelThey met the researchers’ comparison band for this test
74% to 347% of label amountObserved range among samples in which melatonin was detected
One product without detectable melatoninA result reported separately from the detectable range

“Percent of” and “percent more” are different

At 347% of the label, the test measured 3.47 times the amount declared. That is 247% above the declaration. At 74%, the measurement is 26% below the declaration. The paper’s 10% cutoff defines how the researchers counted a match. It is separate from a safety threshold or a legal determination about a specific product.

What an ingredient test cannot answer

Researchers tested gummies sold in the U.S. and compared measured amounts with declared amounts. The study was not a trial of sleep outcomes. It cannot establish the composition of a different lot or the form used in another product. Use the label for what the manufacturer declares. Evidence of actual content requires an appropriate test of the product and sample. The Supplement Facts guide explains how to check whether an amount is per gummy or for multiple gummies.
