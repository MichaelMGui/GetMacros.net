A visible change in puffiness cannot measure body fat. A photograph records a surface under particular conditions, and a scale reports total weight. An estimate of body composition uses additional measurement and assumptions. Studies that changed hydration illustrate why those assumptions matter.

Three observations with different meanings
ObservationQuestion it can answer
Different outline in a photographWhether the visible outline differs under the capture conditions.
Different scale weightWhether total measured mass differed at the two times.
Different compartment estimateWhat the instrument and model estimated with the applicable conditions and assumptions.

Match measurement conditions before interpreting a trend

The cited studies examined how changes in hydration affect body-composition estimates. A reading taken in different conditions can move without isolating a change in fat. That matters even when a device gives a precise-looking percentage. A change in facial appearance supplies less information still.
