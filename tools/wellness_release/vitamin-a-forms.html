The NIH food table gives 6,582 micrograms of retinol activity equivalents, or mcg RAE, for three ounces of pan-fried beef liver. A whole sweet potato baked in its skin lists 1,403 mcg RAE. The common unit accounts for different vitamin A sources. Liver contains predominantly preformed vitamin A, while the sweet potato contributes carotenoids that can be converted in the body.

Use the stated preparation and serving

In this reference comparison, the liver figure is about 4.7 times the sweet potato figure at the listed three-ounce portion. At a one-ounce portion it is about 1.6 times the sweet potato figure. A part of a serving changes the ratio. These are calculations from a table, not matched measurements of every cut or vegetable size.

Keep the form visible

RAE brings the values onto one accounting basis. It does not make a microgram of food beta-carotene the same as a microgram of retinol before conversion. If a table already gives mcg RAE, do not apply the conversion again. NIH’s upper-limit discussion for preformed vitamin A concerns a different question from comparing nutrient amounts.
