A Finnish study followed 2,315 middle-aged men and compared reported sauna habits with later outcomes. The researchers recorded 190 sudden cardiac deaths. The proportion was lower among men who reported more frequent sauna use. The researchers observed their existing habits.

Read the comparison group, then the claim

The first and last groups differ by 5.1 percentage points in their observed proportions. This is a comparison with weekly use. Group sizes also differ, which is why the middle group has more events and a lower percentage. A later analysis from the same cohort explored cardiorespiratory fitness alongside sauna frequency. It is additional context, rather than separate randomized evidence.

Differences in health and habits can remain after statistical adjustment. The result does not say what would happen if a particular person started or increased sauna bathing. For a shorter experiment with a very different outcome, see how one study measured heat strain.
