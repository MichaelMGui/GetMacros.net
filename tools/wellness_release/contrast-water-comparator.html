If alternating warm and cold water is compared with sitting still, any difference could come from the water, the temperature, the alternation or expectations. A study needs more than one comparison condition to narrow down which part mattered.

Check which outcome changed, and for how long

A study of 41 trained men used temperate water, cold water, contrast water and passive rest after exercise. The authors highlighted different short-term performance outcomes for cold and contrast. Another experiment reported better perceived recovery after contrast water at one hour, with no differences from control at 24 or 48 hours on its reported performance or perception measures.

A later contrast-shower study compared the intervention with a neutral shower. It reported better perceived recovery at the end of the intervention and no clear difference in morning cortisol. The result did not persist in follow-up. Showering differs from immersion. Read the comparison, timing and measure together before applying a broad claim to the study.
