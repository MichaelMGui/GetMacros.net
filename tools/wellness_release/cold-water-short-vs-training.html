A short-term change after exercise and an adaptation after weeks of training are different outcomes. A controlled study can test how participants feel or perform after a strenuous session. A long training study can ask whether repeated recovery routines change what participants gain from doing many sessions.

Keep the endpoint tied to the question

Roberts and colleagues studied sustained strength training with cold-water immersion versus active recovery. They reported smaller gains for some adaptation outcomes with the immersion protocol. A separate parallel trial looked at markers and muscle function following exercise and measured different short-term results. The studies need not agree on a single verdict because they asked different questions.

Look for training type, the comparator, and the duration before applying any result to a new setting. Read why the timing of a soreness check matters even in shorter studies.
