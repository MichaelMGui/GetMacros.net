In one night-shift study, both groups averaged about 6.6 to 6.7 hours of sleep in the same daytime window. Their average REM sleep differed by about 29 minutes. The groups were defined by the timing of a biological marker relative to bedtime, rather than by different clock-time sleep opportunities.

Identify how the groups were formed

The study used the peak timing of a urinary melatonin metabolite to estimate phase. The groups also slept different amounts in the week before the test. These differences matter before attributing every change to circadian timing. A separate simulated shift study changed lighting across four nights and measured vigilance and sleep. It gives a different type of evidence because it changed one feature of the schedule.
