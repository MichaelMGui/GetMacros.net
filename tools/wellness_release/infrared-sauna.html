In one study of 16 male basketball players, participants reported lower soreness after infrared sauna than after seated recovery. They also had a smaller drop in one jump test. Those findings cover one form of heat exposure after one type of workout. They do not establish how steam rooms or other saunas affect recovery.

Check which part of recovery the study measured

The study used a crossover design. Each participant went through the workout and the two recovery conditions on separate visits. The researchers assessed performance 14 hours after exercise with multiple tests, including a jump, a short sprint and an isometric leg press. They also recorded subjective soreness and perceived recovery. These measures can tell different stories about the same session.

How to read the word “recovery” in a sauna headline
If the headline saysCheck which outcome actually changed
“Felt recovered”A soreness rating or a report of perceived recovery.
“Performed better the next day”The specific performance test, including the time since the workout and what the control group did.
“Built more muscle over time”A separate long-term comparison with a measure of muscle size. A next-day jump cannot answer that question.

Compare like with like when reading another study. An infrared chamber and a steam room expose people to different conditions. Even trials using the same kind of heat can differ in training load, follow-up time and their control activity. The published result is not a protocol to copy. CDC identifies dehydration and some chronic conditions as factors that can increase the risks of heat exposure.
