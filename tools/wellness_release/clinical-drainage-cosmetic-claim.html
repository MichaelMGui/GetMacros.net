In clinical care, manual lymphatic drainage can be part of a program used for diagnosed lymphedema. It appears in the NCI discussion alongside other components of care. A beauty claim about a changed facial outline answers a different question. A before-and-after photo alone cannot show that lymph moved or that a diagnosed condition improved.

Questions to ask about a facial drainage claim
ContextWhat evidence would be relevant
Clinical programA defined diagnosis, qualified assessment, and documented outcomes under the care plan.
Short-term cosmetic appearanceMatched image conditions and a clearly specified visual outcome.
Claim about fluid itselfA relevant measure of fluid or swelling, independent of photograph appearance.

Look at the treatment context before borrowing a result

A clinical program may combine assessment and multiple treatments. Quoting a program’s result does not establish which one step caused the change. A study of people with a defined medical condition also cannot establish what an at-home cosmetic routine does for someone without that condition.
