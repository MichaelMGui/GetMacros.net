Record observations shortly after you get up. The Consensus Sleep Diary separates when you got into bed, when you tried to sleep, how long you think it took, awakenings, and your view of how the night went. Each answers a different question. You can keep those columns without turning them into a daily grade.

Keep the level of precision honest

In this example the diary suggests about 7 hours 5 minutes asleep, using rough estimates. It does not verify sleep stages. Keep “about” beside a guess and leave an uncertain entry blank. A comparison of the diary with EEG, an activity tracker and a retrospective questionnaire shows why methods should be named when interpreting a report.

Review recurring patterns instead of optimizing the arithmetic from a single night. If you are comparing habits such as caffeine timing, record the actual time and amount consumed. The caffeine timing research guide shows why both details matter.
