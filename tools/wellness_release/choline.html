One large hard-boiled egg supplies 147 mg of choline in the NIH food table. Half a cup of roasted mature soybeans supplies 107 mg, and one cup of cooked quinoa supplies 43 mg. Those three numbers describe different foods and different servings.

What a bowl can add up to

A bowl with one cup of the cooked quinoa and a quarter cup of the roasted soybeans adds up to about 97 mg of choline. Here is the calculation: 43 mg plus half of 107 mg gives 96.5 mg before rounding. Add the egg and the same calculation gives about 244 mg. Treat those as estimates from printed reference values rather than a measurement of the bowl.

Check which soy food the entry describes

The soybean value above is for roasted soybeans. Boiled edamame, tofu and soymilk have their own preparations and water content. The amount in a volume of one product cannot establish the amount in the same volume of another. For a mixed dish, start with explicit ingredient portions before adding their values.

Why the food label may be quiet about choline

In the United States, the FDA Daily Value for choline is 550 mg for adults and children four and older. It is a label reference. The NIH table lists recommended intake amounts separately by age and life stage. A U.S. label generally does not need to list choline unless it was added. If the diary record lacks choline, leave that amount unknown instead of entering a zero.
