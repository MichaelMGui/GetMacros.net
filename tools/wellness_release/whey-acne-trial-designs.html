Two 2024 papers asked different questions about whey and acne. One compared previous whey use among people with and without acne. The other assigned different supplements to a small group of men who already had acne and measured changes over six months.

Read what the trial was designed to rule out

A noninferiority design asks whether an outcome with the test product is worse than the comparison beyond a defined margin. The trial authors reported a noninferior difference in changes within their sample. That conclusion does not say that taking whey treated acne, or that it had exactly the same effect for each participant. The case-control study addresses existing exposures and cannot assign whey as the cause of the differences it observed.

Apply the result to the population and preparation studied. A different supplement or a person starting without acne asks a different question. For a second example, read how to interpret milk-and-acne questionnaires.
