“As packaged” describes the food before you add anything at home. “As prepared” describes a specific finished preparation. Use the prepared figure when it matches the directions you followed. If you use different additions, start with the packaged amounts and add what went into your pan.

What counts in a U.S. boxed macaroni example
BasisWhat the number can coverWhat to check
As packagedThe dry pasta and cheese mix supplied in the boxUse the amount of dry mix you used, not the cooked weight

A changed recipePackaged ingredients plus your actual additionsUse the fraction of the finished batch you served

A recipe yield and a nutrition serving can differ

Kraft’s U.S. recipe for a 7.25 oz box adds a quarter-cup each of margarine and milk and describes three recipe servings. Its product page uses a dry serving of 62 g, described as making about a cup, and gives an approximate label serving count. Use your package and actual yield instead of treating the recipe’s three bowls as three printed servings.

For a changed recipe, enter the package and each addition once. Weigh the finished dish and your serving if you want to use their ratio. If you instead use a prepared figure that already includes the milk and margarine, adding them again counts them twice. The food diary lets you keep the recipe and served portion together.
