Examples of foods with vitamin B12 and no dairy include cooked Atlantic salmon and cooked clams. Fortified foods can also provide B12. The useful label word is “fortified,” and the amount varies by product.

Compare two cartons at the same amount

Suppose label A lists 1.2 mcg per 250 mL and label B lists 1.0 mcg per 200 mL. At 200 mL, label A’s proportional amount is 0.96 mcg. The two hypothetical labels look different per serving but come very close once volume matches. Check the actual labels when B12 is the reason for comparing. The NIH yeast range is brand-specific, and product information should establish whether a yeast is fortified at all.

Check what “without dairy” means for you

Seafood contains no dairy by itself, but a prepared product may have additional ingredients. If you also avoid animal foods, the seafood rows do not apply to that choice. Start with foods explicitly fortified with B12 and use the declared amount. These tables compare food content. They do not address individual absorption or diagnose a deficiency.
