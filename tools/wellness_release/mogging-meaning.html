In internet slang, “mogging” means outshining someone or something. In discussions of appearance, a commenter may use the word to claim that one person looks better than another. Collins chose it as its 2026 Word of the Year and describes broader and sometimes joking uses too. The word expresses a comparison, which is different from a measurement of health or attractiveness.

Read what the word is doing in the sentence

Three invented uses of the same slang
ExampleWhat it communicates
“That loaf mogs mine.”The speaker is joking that another result outshines their own.
“She mogs everyone in this photo.”The commenter is ranking appearances. The ranking is their judgment.
“Eat this to start mogging.”A food claim is being tied to social comparison. It needs a defined outcome and supporting evidence.

Check the claim hidden in the punchline

If a post promises that a food or supplement will change how you look, ask what outcome is supposed to change. A photograph, a measured change in skin hydration and a treatment result are different kinds of evidence. Search for the outcome in the original study. If only the caption links the product to the claimed effect, the slang has done the work the evidence would need to do.

Looksmaxxing is a broader term for attempts to alter or improve appearance. Mogging describes the comparison someone is making. Knowing the difference helps you read the post without taking an internet ranking as a reason to change what you eat.
