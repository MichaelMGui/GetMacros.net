A blood or saliva sample collected immediately after exercise can reflect the exercise itself. To interpret a result in a study, check when it was collected relative to the workout, how hard the activity was and what happened to the control sample.

A rising concentration can have several contributors

The 12 moderately trained men in the study completed all four conditions on separate days. The low-intensity increase was not statistically significant. The researchers also considered plasma volume, because a smaller fluid volume can change a concentration reading. These are study means for a particular sampling window, rather than values a person should expect after a workout.

Other protocols collected blood at the end of successive three-minute workloads or measured saliva during up to an hour of cycling. They use different timing, samples and workload designs. Check those details before turning one study’s intensity into a universal threshold. For a clinical test, follow the timing instructions from the ordering care team.
