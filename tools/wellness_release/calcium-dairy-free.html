In the NIH table, one cup of calcium-fortified soymilk and one cup of nonfat cow’s milk each supply about 299 mg calcium. Half a cup of firm calcium-sulfate tofu supplies 253 mg. The qualification matters. “Soy” and “tofu” by themselves do not tell you how much calcium is in the package.

What if you use only a splash?

If you use a quarter cup of the fortified soymilk, a proportional estimate is about 75 mg using the table’s stated cup. Add the half cup of the specified tofu and the subtotal is about 328 mg. The calculation explains how portions add up. It does not imply that other brands have the same amounts or that the body absorbs the same amount from the subtotal.

Take the label with you

Check the calcium line and serving volume on a plant beverage rather than copying the table value to every carton. For tofu, look at both the calcium declaration and the coagulant on the ingredient list. Two products can be firm and still use different ingredients. HealthLink BC gives a range for calcium-sulfate tofu rather than a single figure, which shows why the exact package matters.
