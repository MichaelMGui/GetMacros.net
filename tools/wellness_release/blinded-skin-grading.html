A blinded skin assessment withholds group or time-point information from the person grading the outcome. In an Australian study of sunscreen and beta-carotene, the graders assessing skin surface measurements did not know treatment assignment. A reader should check who was blinded and what material they saw.

Separate what was assigned from what was scored

The Australian trial assigned sunscreen use and beta-carotene versus placebo separately. It had 903 adult participants and assessed change in the tiny surface relief of the skin between 1992 and 1996. The graders did not know the product allocation. The study found no overall effect for beta-carotene on the measured outcome. Some outcome data were missing. Grader blinding helps with scoring bias but cannot recover those missing measurements.
