In the U.S., the FDA accepts reports of suspected problems from dietary supplements through its Safety Reporting Portal. The FDA advises contacting a healthcare provider first for a suspected reaction or illness. You can still report a concern if the role of the product is uncertain.

Information that helps identify a suspected problem
Information to retainWhy it helps
Exact product, maker, label and lotDistinguishes versions with similar names
When you used it and when the problem beganDescribes the sequence without assuming causation

Why reports are signals rather than rates

Suppose a search returns more reports for product A than product B. The count does not show how many people used each product, how often events were reported or whether the reports established a cause. The FDA’s documentation for the public files says the reports cannot estimate incidence or risk. The reporting route is for documenting an event, while the public table describes what people reported.

Use the official link in the FDA’s reporting guidance when you need to file. The Supplement Facts guide can help identify the product and serving from the package.
