Two studies of magnesium and sleep recruited different groups. One enrolled 46 older adults in a trial of primary insomnia. Another enrolled 155 people aged 18–65 who reported poor sleep quality. Those descriptions affect who the results can reasonably represent.

Look for three pieces of eligibility context

Check the age range, how the sleep complaint was defined, and whether the paper excluded other conditions or medicines. A diagnosis and a self-reported sleep complaint are different ways to recruit. A change in a questionnaire can matter while leaving other outcomes, such as sleep-stage measurements, unresolved.

If a headline says “works for insomnia,” start with the population and the actual control group. Read the placebo comparison for what changed in both arms of the newer study.
