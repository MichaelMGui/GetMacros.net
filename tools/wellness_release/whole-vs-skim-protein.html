The Organic Valley whole and skim half-gallons both list 8 g of protein per cup. Their energy differs: 150 calories per cup for the whole milk and 90 for the skim. At the same cup size the protein ties. At equal energy the skim supplies more protein in this pair because the calculation counts a larger volume.

Decide whether the glass or the calorie count is fixed

For a 120 mL pour of either milk, the label gives an estimate of 4 g protein. For a 100-calorie comparison, the two volumes are about 160 mL whole and 267 mL skim. Use the per-cup line if you’re replacing an equal volume in a recipe. The density comparison answers a separate question.

Use the package that matches the pour

These are ordinary whole and skim products from one brand. A high-protein, filtered or flavored carton may supply different amounts. The calories and nutrient amounts are rounded on the package; display calculations inherit that rounding. The matched 2% milk comparison shows what happens when fat stays fixed and the type of processing changes.
