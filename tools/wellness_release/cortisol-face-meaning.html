“Cortisol face” is an informal phrase used in posts that attribute facial fullness or puffiness to cortisol. A photo can show an appearance under particular conditions. It cannot measure a hormone, show whether a change is persistent, or identify its cause.

Three different things a photo caption can combine
ClaimEvidence it would need
A face looks different in two imagesComparable lighting, distance, pose and camera setup
The person’s cortisol was elevatedClinical context and appropriate measurements
Cortisol caused a changeClinical assessment of the timing, findings, test results and possible causes

A possible clinical feature is only part of an assessment

NIDDK lists a rounded face among several possible features of Cushing’s syndrome, which involves prolonged excess exposure to cortisol. The presence of a feature on a list cannot establish the cause of a person’s appearance. The institute describes a diagnosis using medical history, examination and lab tests. Endocrine Society guidance likewise relies on a clinical assessment and appropriate testing.

Read the claim rather than the caption

A post might show two photos and say daily stress caused a hormonal condition. The images cannot supply the missing measurements. If the issue is a change in your own health, a healthcare professional can assess it in context.
