A meal diary can show what its author chose to record. It cannot establish why their skin changed. Knowing when the change started, what the skin looked like before that, and which meals or days were left out matters before reading causal meaning into a post.

How to audit a three-week diary claim
CheckExample of missing information
Time orderDid the claimed dietary pattern actually begin before the visible change?
Baseline trendWere observations made long enough before the change to tell whether the outcome had already moved?
Full observation windowWere ordinary days included, or only the meals and photos selected for the post?

Compare the record with the strength of the claim

Published observational research can document associations using consistent questions and a larger group. It still has limitations, including how well past diet is captured. A one-person retrospective post supplies fewer controls and usually no counterfactual. A diary can be useful for recording what happened without identifying the cause.
