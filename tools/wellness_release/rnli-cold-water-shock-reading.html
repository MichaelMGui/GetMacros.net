Cold-water shock can affect breathing and movement immediately after entering cold water. Gradual body cooling is a different problem that can develop with continued exposure. Looking only for signs of prolonged cooling misses the immediate risk when someone unexpectedly enters the water.

Read the threshold with its context

RNLI flags water around 15°C and below for cold-water shock. That figure is a water-safety benchmark for describing a hazard. It cannot certify an individual exposure, and conditions such as currents and difficult exits matter separately. A video of someone tolerating a supervised bath cannot answer what happens during accidental entry in open water.
