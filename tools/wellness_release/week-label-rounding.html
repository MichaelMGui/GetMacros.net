Seven identical servings multiply a per-serving rounding difference by seven. For U.S. added-sugar labels expressed to the nearest gram, a difference of 0.4 g between a measured amount and its printed integer becomes 2.8 g across seven servings.

When the same error repeats

Each serving of example A logs 0.4 g more than the invented measured amount. Example B logs 0.4 g less. When you eat the same product and portion repeatedly, the rounding has the same direction. That is why the two examples can produce the same diary total and have different underlying totals. Ordinary manufacturing and portion variation are separate sources of uncertainty.

Leave the extra precision in the example

The exact unrounded amount on a real product generally is not printed. Use the declared amount and your actual serving in the diary. FDA also gives conditions for using zero or a less-than-one-gram statement for very small quantities. Those cases have separate rules. The examples here illustrate the ordinary nearest-gram calculation around 5 g, not those exceptions.
