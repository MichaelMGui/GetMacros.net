One study found lower soreness after cold water compared with no immersion following shuttle running. Another found little difference across most outcomes when ice water was compared with tepid water following eccentric leg exercise. The two controls answer different questions.

What differed between the published trials
ComparisonActivity and controlSoreness result
Bailey and colleagues, 20 participantsProlonged intermittent shuttle running. Cold exposure versus no immersion.Lower rated soreness in the cold group at 1, 24 and 48 hours.
Sellwood and colleagues, 40 participantsUnfamiliar eccentric leg loading. Ice water versus tepid water.Most pain measures were similar. The ice group reported a larger increase in sit-to-stand pain at 24 hours.

Read what was compared before comparing the conclusion

The result from a temperature trial controls for being in water. A comparison with rest leaves that feature open. Also check how people rated soreness. Walking, standing from a chair and rating a resting muscle can produce different results. Comparing headlines loses all three differences.

These protocols also used different exposure lengths and populations. They do not identify a universally best temperature. See how soreness relates to measured strength for the difference between feeling better and producing more force.
