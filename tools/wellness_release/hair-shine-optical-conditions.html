Hair shine depends partly on how a surface reflects light. In a controlled analysis, some applied oils increased measured luster, and hair fibers of different pigmentation produced different readings. A shiny stripe in a photo can tell you about the visible reflection under that light. It cannot show what somebody ate.

Match the claim to a measured outcome

To evaluate a claim about a food, look for research that records or assigns the food exposure and measures a defined outcome under comparable conditions. Changes in luster, counts of new hairs and hair-shaft strength are separate outcomes. Standardized images can help describe one of them when the protocol says how.
