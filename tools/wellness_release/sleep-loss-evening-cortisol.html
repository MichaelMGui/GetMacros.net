A controlled study found higher cortisol in the evening after a restricted night of sleep. The detectable change was in the next evening window, rather than every time point the researchers sampled. That also means “higher the next day” omits the part of the result that tells you when it happened.

Read the percentage against the previous evening

Leproult and colleagues measured plasma across 32 hours in young men. The 37% and 45% figures compare the measured evening after the intervention with the prior evening in the same condition. You cannot use those relative changes to infer someone’s concentration without knowing the baseline, sample timing and test conditions.

The experiments also changed sleep timing in the partial-deprivation condition. The results describe acute laboratory schedules and do not estimate an individual effect after a night that was only slightly shorter. See how time in bed differs from time asleep when reading the reported sleep window.
