A stress rating describes what someone reports feeling. A cortisol sample measures one part of a physiological response at a specific time. A person can report feeling very stressed without showing the same cortisol pattern as someone with the same rating.

Where the two types of measurement can differ in a lab task
When it is measuredWhat a person can reportWhat a cortisol comparison can ask
Before the taskHow tense or worried they feel about doing itWhether cortisol already changed during anticipation
During a task or immediately afterwardHow the experience felt while it was happeningWhat the sample captures under that schedule, including earlier anticipation
During recoveryHow they feel now that the task has finishedWhether a later sample reflects a change that followed the task

Check the time window before calling the measurements inconsistent

Engert and colleagues studied anticipatory and reactive cortisol around a lab stressor. They reported that visible reactive changes emerged after it ended. They did not find an association between anticipatory cortisol and their subjective stress response measures. That result applies to the measured conditions and does not say that feelings or hormone measurements are meaningless.

If two headline numbers seem to disagree, check what the questionnaire asked, when the sample was collected and whether the study tested the relationship between them. See another example of how sampling time changes a cortisol result.
