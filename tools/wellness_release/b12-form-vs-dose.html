The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements reports no evidence that vitamin B12 absorption rates from supplements vary by form. The amount taken affects the fraction absorbed. A smaller percentage of a larger amount can still be more B12 in absolute terms.

Two NIH reference examples, with calculated amounts
Example oral amountApproximate fraction absorbedImplied absolute amount
500 mcg2%500 × 0.02 ≈ 10 mcg
1,000 mcg1.3%1,000 × 0.013 ≈ 13 mcg

Read the dose before reading the tagline

The comparison above varies the amount. To test whether a product’s form is absorbed better, look for a head-to-head study with matched amounts and a clearly stated outcome. The name methylcobalamin or cyanocobalamin identifies a form on the label. It does not disclose how a specific person will absorb the product.

Check the amount per serving and how many units make up the serving. The arithmetic above uses approximate population figures to illustrate a percentage. It cannot predict a personal result or choose a supplement regimen. For a quick check of the serving line, use the Supplement Facts walkthrough.
