Almonds, grilled portabella mushrooms, eggs and fortified grain foods can provide riboflavin without a glass of cow’s milk. In the NIH table, one ounce of dry-roasted almonds lists 0.3 mg and a cup of cooked quinoa lists 0.2 mg. Together they reach the 0.5 mg listed for a cup of 2% milk in the same table.

Check the amount you put in the bowl

With a half-ounce topping of almonds, the quinoa combination would give about 0.35 mg using the printed amounts. That estimate comes from halving the 0.3 mg and adding 0.2 mg. It describes riboflavin only. Calcium, protein and other nutrients can differ between the foods.

Inspect the oat label before using the fortified figure

Fortified instant oats are a particular entry. Another plain or unfortified product can have different amounts. When a package gives riboflavin or identifies added vitamin B2, compare the label’s serving and prepared amount.
