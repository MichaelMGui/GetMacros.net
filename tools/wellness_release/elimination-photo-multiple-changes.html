If someone changes a food, changes skincare and takes the second photo in a different room, the later image reflects a new set of conditions. It cannot isolate what the food did. Even when one visible difference is real, several explanations can remain.

A hypothetical timeline with three changes
Change after the first photoWhat it leaves open
Switched to a different breakfastMore than one ingredient and portion may have changed together.
Started a different skincare routineAny result could also reflect the new routine or its timing.
Used a new place to take the photoLighting and camera geometry may differ even if the skin did not.

Ask what the observation was designed to compare

A trial that intends to test one component would define the intervention, keep the rest of the comparison comparable and set a consistent outcome. A photo timeline made after several spontaneous changes does not have those features. Describe the observation as an association with the bundle of changes rather than a demonstrated effect of one ingredient.
