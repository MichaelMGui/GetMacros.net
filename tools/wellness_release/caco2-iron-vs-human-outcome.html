An in vitro result describes a laboratory model. Iron bioavailability studies can ask how an intestinal cell model responds to prepared samples, or they can measure iron absorption in people. A study of maize and beans compared predictions with human measurements, which is a much more direct test of how the methods line up than comparing different foods across unrelated studies.

Three outcomes in an iron claim
OutcomeWhat it can tell you
Cell-model responseHow the assay responded to the tested preparation under laboratory conditions.
Human measured absorptionHow the tested meal’s iron was absorbed by enrolled participants under study conditions.
Longer-term iron status or symptomsRequires a separate outcome and follow-up appropriate to the clinical question.

Keep the food preparation in the summary

The tested matrix and meal ingredients can matter. A result from one bean preparation does not assign an absorption percentage to all beans. Even a human absorption measurement does not tell you whether an individual’s symptoms will change.
