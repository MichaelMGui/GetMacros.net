Know what the interval can answer

The interval and examples apply to a proportion under the stated sampling assumptions. They do not address biased recruiting, dropouts, poor controls or a loose definition of improvement. A large result can still be misleading if it counts only the people who completed the program or changes the endpoint after looking at the data.

Ask for the numerator, denominator, time point and comparison group. If the two groups show similar improvement at the same follow-up, the single-group percentage tells only part of the story. See how skin research defines outcomes before comparing percentages of people who supposedly improved.
