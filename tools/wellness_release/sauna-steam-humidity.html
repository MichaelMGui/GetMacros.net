Air temperature is only part of the heat load. Higher humidity can limit how readily sweat evaporates. In a small dry sauna and steam bath comparison, participants lost more weight in the dry sauna, while the steam bath produced the higher physiological strain score.

Separate the two pieces of evidence

The sauna study ran the dry condition first and the steam bath a month later. The air conditions differed in both humidity and temperature, so its figures describe those specific exposures. A separate experiment kept air at 35°C while varying humidity during walking. It found that reduced evaporation at higher humidity could leave participants with a greater heat load. The latter isolated humidity more directly, under exercise conditions.

When assessing a comparison, write down the air temperature, humidity, exposure length and how researchers measured strain. A short-term drop on the scale cannot establish fat loss. See what an infrared sauna recovery study measured for an example with a different outcome.
