“Glow” needs a definition before it can be compared across studies. It might refer to how bright an image is, how a surface reflects light, or what observers perceive as attractive. An original skin-gloss study distinguished optical properties and preferred versus unpreferred gloss, which is more specific than a single promotional label.

Three outcomes that may get called “glow”
Outcome describedQuestion the evidence answers
Instrument glossWhat reflection pattern did the device record at the selected angle and site?
Color or luminanceWhat changed on a particular measured color or lightness axis?
Observer preferenceWhich images did these observers prefer under the study’s conditions?

Ask what the advertised percentage measures

A benefit phrased as “more glow” should tell you the instrument or rating, when it was measured and what it was compared with. The smoothie study, for example, reported separate lightness and color axes. Its reported color changes do not establish a gloss result or tell you how much a consumer will like their appearance.
