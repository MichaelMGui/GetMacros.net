Acne studies can count individual lesions and grade overall appearance separately. The U.S. FDA’s final drug-trial guidance explains why both measures are useful. A count can show a change in the number of lesions. A severity grade describes what an assessor sees across the area using predefined categories.

Three outcomes a reader might see in a skin study
Reported outcomeWhat was measuredDetails to look up
Lesion countA tally. The FDA recommends counting inflammatory and noninflammatory facial lesions separately in the drug trials covered by its guidance.Starting count, change in the count, lesion type and comparison group.
Investigator’s Global AssessmentAn ordinal assessment of severity, based on the appearance as a whole.How the study defines each grade and what it considers a successful change.
Participant-rated improvementThe participant’s experience or impression, when the study reports it.Question wording, when it was asked, and whether participants knew their assignment.

Read percentages alongside starting counts

A fictional 50% reduction could describe a drop from 4 to 2 or from 20 to 10. That gives two different absolute changes. A change in a count also cannot be converted directly to a shift in an ordinal grade. A trial can report both outcomes and the comparison group’s change so a reader can see how they relate.
