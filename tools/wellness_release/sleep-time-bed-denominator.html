Time in bed can include reading, waiting to fall asleep and being awake during the night. Time asleep is the portion actually counted as sleep. A sleep-efficiency percentage also needs a denominator. Two definitions can give different-looking scores for the same night.

Divide 430 by 510 and the example is about 84%. Divide 430 by 480 and it is about 90%. Reed and Sacco discuss why activities like reading can make the literal time-in-bed denominator less useful for a question about attempting to sleep. The example assumes no extra awake time in bed after deciding to get up.

A diary, a wearable and an EEG can estimate the sleep window differently. For a weekly comparison, use the same method and definition each night. For a device comparison, read its definitions before taking the difference as a change in your sleep. See how trackers estimate sleep stages.
