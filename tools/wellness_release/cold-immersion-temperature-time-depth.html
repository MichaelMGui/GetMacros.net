Water temperature describes the water. A cold-immersion experiment also needs to say how long people were immersed, how much of the body was in the water and where the researchers measured cooling. A temperature reading by itself cannot specify tissue temperature or how a person will respond.

What to write beside the temperature in an immersion study
DetailQuestion it answers
Exposure timeFor how long was heat transferred during that trial?
Immersed areaHow much of the body was in contact with the water?
Measurement site and timeDid the study measure muscle, skin or core temperature, and did it sample during immersion or afterward?

Separate cooling from the claimed benefit

A study of settings for lowering muscle temperature can help interpret cooling under its protocol. It does not show that another bath improved exercise performance or sleep. Even two trials using the same water temperature can differ if their measurement site, depth or duration differs.

Cool-water research findings also cannot establish an individual safety cutoff. RNLI explains the immediate breathing and movement risks of unexpected cold immersion. See more examples of separating an exposure from its outcomes.
