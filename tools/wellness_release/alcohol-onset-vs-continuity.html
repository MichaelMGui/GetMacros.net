A laboratory study found different effects of alcohol at different points in the night. Sleep was more consolidated in the first half and less consolidated in the second half compared with the placebo condition. An early-night observation therefore does not describe how well someone slept across the night.

Which part of sleep is the headline talking about?
MeasurementWhat it tells you
Time to fall asleepHow long sleep took to start under the recording conditions
Sleep consolidationHow much sleep stayed continuous during a defined interval
Morning sleep-quality ratingHow the participant described the night afterwards

Divide the night before interpreting an average

Two nights could have the same total minutes awake but place those minutes in different halves. One number for the entire night would hide that change. Likewise, a result about falling asleep cannot tell you how often sleep was interrupted afterwards. Arnedt and colleagues used lab recordings and also gathered morning ratings, which were lower after alcohol in their study.

Read the experimental conditions

The investigators studied healthy young adults and compared an alcohol-intoxication condition with placebo under supervision. That design can isolate some differences between the two conditions. It cannot predict an individual response or establish the effect of every drinking pattern. If a headline says a drink “helps sleep,” check which outcome improved and which period it describes.
