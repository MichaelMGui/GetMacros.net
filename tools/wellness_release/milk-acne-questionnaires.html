Milk-and-acne questionnaires can estimate whether people with different reported intake also reported different rates of acne. They cannot show what would happen to one person after changing one food. The time of reporting, who answered and the comparison group all matter.

What 1.19 means here

The ratio measures relative prevalence between the compared groups after the study’s adjustments. A value of 1.19 is about 19% higher prevalence relative to the group with lower intake, rather than an increase of 19 percentage points. The girls’ study described its highest total milk category as at least two servings a day. The boys’ study used more than two a day. Check the definitions before treating the two groups as identical.

Both papers used reported intake and a reported skin outcome. Even an adjusted comparison can retain differences in lifestyle, reporting and other exposures. A trial that allocates one ingredient answers a different question. The whey research comparison shows that distinction in practice.
