A medium raw apple with skin provides about 4.4 g of fiber in USDA reference data. The peeled medium-apple entry works out to about 2.1 g. The skin-on reference portion is heavier and has more fiber at the same weight.

Match the preparation when you weigh the slices

If both portions weigh 150 g on your scale, the estimates become 3.6 g with skin and about 2.0 g without skin. The difference is smaller here than when comparing two typical whole fruit entries. The 182 g and 161 g weights are reference portions of the same named size, so subtracting them does not give a precise peel weight for one apple.

When you log slices or a recipe, enter the actual edible weight and select the record for skin included or removed. Use the protein and fiber comparison to see how the skin-on entry changes when you switch measurement bases.
