The NIH food table lists 629 mcg copper per ounce of dry-roasted cashews and 615 mcg per quarter cup of toasted sunflower seed kernels. Those reference servings supply nearly the same amount. A tablespoon of the sunflower kernels supplies about a quarter of their listed copper.

A half-ounce of cashews contributes less than a full quarter cup of the seeds. In a meal with a tablespoon of seeds instead, the cashews would contribute more. The ranking changes with how much you use.

Compare a weighed amount when precision matters

An ounce is a weight and a cup is a volume. For an equal-gram comparison, look up a gram weight for that exact kernel preparation instead of applying the weight of a different seed. Use the diary to retain the portion you actually measured.
