A half cup of boiled, drained lentils has about 3 mg iron in the NIH table. Three ounces of braised, trimmed beef bottom round has about 2 mg. Beef liver, a different food, supplies about 5 mg in a three-ounce pan-fried portion. Compare the actual foods before concluding that “beef” has one iron figure.

Compare the plate before the category

A bowl with half a cup each of the lentils and chickpeas adds up to about 5 mg from the two printed rows. That happens to match the listed iron in one liver portion. If you ate only a quarter cup of the lentils, the proportional estimate from the same row would be 1.5 mg. These sums use rounded reference values and leave out the rest of the meal.

A milligram on the table is content

NIH distinguishes heme and nonheme iron. Plant foods contain nonheme iron. Meat supplies a mix, including heme iron, which is generally more bioavailable. The equal printed totals above therefore cannot establish how much iron someone absorbs from either meal. The rest of the diet and the individual also matter. Use the table for food content and keep any diagnosis or treatment discussion separate.
