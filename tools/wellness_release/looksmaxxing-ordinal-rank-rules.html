A chart that ranks faces puts them in an order chosen by the writer or raters. It cannot show how far apart the ranks are or whether other people would use the same scale. The numbers often look more precise than the judgment that produced them.

Three checks for reading appearance rankings
AskWhat the answer would clarify
Who made the scale?Whether the order comes from one creator, multiple raters or a defined measurement method.
What does one step represent?Whether going from rank two to three describes a countable change or only a position in an order.
Does it work with different people and images?Whether there is evidence that raters agree under conditions beyond the examples used to build it.

Separate the chart from research on how people encounter it

Ofcom’s research describes how people in its qualitative sample engaged with online material. It does not establish an objective appearance hierarchy. A validated clinical skin scale addresses a defined feature with specified grading conditions, which also cannot establish broad personal appeal or social value.
