If you have a raw label and weigh the whole cooked batch, you can estimate a serving as a share of that batch. Dividing cooked serving grams by the raw serving size usually puts two different weights in the same calculation.

Example using a hypothetical raw label and plain batch
StepValue to record
Raw package400 g with four 100 g servings at 20 g protein each, for 80 g protein entered.
Cooked yield300 g weighed after cooking.
Portion served150 g cooked, one-half of the cooked batch, for about 40 g of the entered protein.

Use a match for what was kept

The example assumes the portion represents the cooked batch evenly and treats the recorded protein as retained. If the recipe leaves out rendered fat, drained liquid or scraps, applying the entire raw package’s nutrients to the remaining weight can overstate some nutrients. USDA retention and yield work records losses differently across foods and preparations.

Use a suitable cooked and drained reference if it more closely represents the serving. Avoid logging both a cooked entry and a separate share of the same raw ingredients. See the cooked-yield method applied to rice.
