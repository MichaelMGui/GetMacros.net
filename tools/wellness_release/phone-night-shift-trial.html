In a seven-night trial of 167 people ages 18 to 24, researchers found no statistically significant difference across the full sample in the sleep outcomes they tracked for Night Shift on, Night Shift off, and no phone use. The comparison tested a particular setting and behavior before bed. It did not establish that any phone will have the same effect on everyone’s sleep.

Read the outcome before interpreting the headline

The phone trial used wrist accelerometers for estimates such as time to fall asleep and time awake after sleep onset. The separate tablet experiment studied melatonin suppression. An immediate hormone result does not establish how long participants slept that night. The phone trial’s authors also reported an exploratory subgroup analysis after the main analysis. That subgroup result calls for replication and does not replace the finding for the full sample.

Use this checklist when comparing settings in a sleep headline: check whether people actually used a device in both groups, whether brightness was held fixed, and whether the outcome was an estimate of sleep or a laboratory marker. For a broader timing example, see what sleep regularity measures.
