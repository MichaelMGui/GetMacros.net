Milligrams of iron in food describe how much was present. Absorption depends on the form of iron, the rest of the meal and the person. In one comparison of ten meals, nonheme iron content ranged from 3.9 to 7.8 mg, while measured absorbed nonheme iron ranged from 0.33 to 1.80 mg.

Read the range without inventing a pairing

The extreme values in one column need not be the same meals as the extreme values in the other. Dividing the low or high ends to estimate an individual absorption percentage would be unreliable. A separate study checked complete diets over several days, illustrating how a single-meal effect and overall intake can address different questions.

Track the food’s supported milligrams for an intake log and keep absorption separate. The NIH explains that heme and nonheme iron occur in different sources and that many things influence uptake. The Nutrient Density Explorer gives reference food amounts, without estimating absorbed iron.
