A tablespoon of Swanson blackstrap molasses lists 3.6 mg of iron and 130 mg of calcium. Golden Barrel’s manufacturer specification works out to about 2.6 mg of iron and 193 mg of calcium for a weighed 20 g portion. Both products are called blackstrap molasses, so the name alone cannot tell you what to enter in a recipe.

Read the jar before entering the recipe

The Golden Barrel sheet lists 12.9 mg of iron, 965 mg of calcium and 50.3 g of total sugars per 100 g. Multiplying each by 0.20 converts the specification to the weighed portion. The Swanson label gives a volume. The table keeps those bases visible because you need a mass from the product or a scale to turn a tablespoon into grams.

Suppose you use one labeled tablespoon of Swanson in a batch that makes four equal servings. The molasses contributes about 0.9 mg iron, 32.5 mg calcium and 3.5 g total sugars to each. Add the other ingredients separately. For a second jar, use its label and measured portion and recalculate the recipe in the food diary.
