An epigenetic clock is a model that converts marks measured on DNA into an age-related score. The COSMOS substudy assessed such scores from collected blood samples in a randomized trial of a multivitamin–multimineral and cocoa extract. A result on one clock describes that signal under the study conditions. A trial would need separate outcomes to establish a change in lifespan.

What three forms of outcome can support
OutcomeWhat it describes
Age-like scoreThe model’s age-related output at a defined measurement time.
Pace measureA model designed to estimate a rate rather than assign a date of birth or number of remaining years.
Observed health eventAn actual event recorded using clinical criteria and follow-up.

Ask how many clocks were tested

The CALERIE analysis, a separate intervention, illustrates how pace and age-type measures can have different results even when they use samples from the same trial. When a headline quotes one result, check the named clock, prespecified analyses, the other clocks tested and which group it was compared with. A methylation score does not tell an individual how many years they will live.
