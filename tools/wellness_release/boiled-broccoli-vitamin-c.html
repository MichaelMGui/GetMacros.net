In one laboratory comparison, broccoli retained about 53% of its original vitamin C after boiling under the study conditions. The cooked broccoli had 37.0 mg per 100 g, compared with 66.8 mg per 100 g raw. That per-weight comparison comes to about 55%, because cooking also changed the weight of the broccoli.

For a fixed 100 g serving, read the middle column. To follow the vitamin C in the original batch, use the last column, which accounts for the amount of food before and after cooking. A measurement above 100% can occur in a cooking study. It describes the measured amounts under that experiment’s conditions. It does not establish that heating created vitamin C.

What to log

Match the database entry to the food you weighed. Use a cooked, drained entry for cooked, drained broccoli. A separate stock or soup recipe has a different edible portion if you keep the liquid. This experiment provides no nutrient value for that leftover liquid. Try the Nutrient Density Explorer for a reference estimate of the cooked portion.
