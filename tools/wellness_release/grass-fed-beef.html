A 2026 study found a higher share of several omega-3 fatty acids in the fat of sampled North American grass-fed ribeyes than in the grain-fed ribeyes it tested. The study compared meat composition. It did not measure what happened to people’s health when they ate either steak.

Keep “of the fat” separate from “of the steak”

The researchers worked with 253 grass-fed and 84 grain-fed ribeye samples. They reported individual fatty acids as a share of total fatty acids. A piece of meat can have a high proportion of a fatty acid and a smaller amount of total fat. Those are separate numbers, so you cannot translate a proportional difference directly into milligrams per serving.

Two ways to describe the same type of comparison
QuestionWhat you need
What fraction of the fat is EPA or DHA?The fatty-acid proportions within the sample.
How many milligrams are in this portion?The fatty-acid proportions, the fat amount in the portion, and a consistent serving basis.
Does eating this steak improve health outcomes?A human study with an explicit comparison diet and measured outcomes.

What the authors’ example can tell you

The paper illustrates the difference with 100 g of ribeye containing about 12 g of fat in both groups. At that assumed fat content, its calculation gives about 47 mg of EPA plus DHA for grass-fed and 16 mg for grain-fed ribeye, or about 31 mg difference. Those figures are the authors’ standardized calculation from the study. They are not label values for all steaks, and they do not include every omega-3 fat.

When a headline says “three times more,” ask for the underlying serving and the absolute amounts. Then check whether the steak you are considering is the same cut and fat content. This paper cannot assign its averages to an individual package or establish a simple “healthier beef” verdict.
