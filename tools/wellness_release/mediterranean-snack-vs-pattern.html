A package described as Mediterranean describes one item or flavor. In nutrition guidance, Mediterranean-style eating describes choices repeated across meals. The American Heart Association notes that the pattern has regional variation and commonly emphasizes vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes and unsaturated fats such as olive oil.

Start with what the label actually says

Imagine crackers made with olive oil whose grains are mainly refined. The oil is one ingredient. It cannot tell you how much whole grain is in the serving or what other meals look like. A hummus label can confirm its ingredients and sodium per serving. It cannot tell you how often someone eats legumes.

Check what research was studying

To connect a food with a diet study, compare what people actually ate and how the whole pattern was supported. The PREDIMED comparison studied defined interventions, not collections of products carrying one word on the package. Use that distinction to interpret a claim, then use the food comparison tool if your question is about serving-level nutrition.
