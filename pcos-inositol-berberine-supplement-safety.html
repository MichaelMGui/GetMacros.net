PCOS Supplements: Inositol, Berberine and Safety

PCOS supplement shelves are full of confident promises. Evidence, product quality, interactions and pregnancy plans are often much less certain.

Safety boundary: Supplements can interact with glucose, blood-pressure, anticoagulant, fertility and psychiatric medicines. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver or kidney disease needs clinician or pharmacist review.

Inositol: possible, not proven as a cure

The international guideline says inositol in any form could be considered based on preferences, noting limited harm and possible metabolic improvements, but limited clinical benefit for ovulation, hirsutism or weight. It cannot currently recommend a specific type, dose or combination because evidence quality is inadequate.

Look for the exact outcome: period frequency, laboratory value, live birth, symptom score or adverse event. Testimonials and before-and-after photos cannot show whether a product caused the change.

Check quality signals

Prefer a single-ingredient product when possible, clear amounts rather than a proprietary blend, a lot number, and credible independent testing. Third-party testing can support identity and contamination checks; it does not prove effectiveness.

Avoid stacked formulas

Hair, metabolism and fertility blends may duplicate vitamin A, iodine, selenium or stimulants. Biotin can interfere with some laboratory tests. Record brand, dose and frequency, and photograph the Supplement Facts panel.

Set a stop rule

Before a trial, decide what outcome matters, when to reassess and which side effects end the trial. Seek help for severe rash, swelling, breathing trouble, jaundice, persistent vomiting, fainting or signs of low blood glucose.
