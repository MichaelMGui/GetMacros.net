A bulk pack saves money only if you use enough of it

Your shelf

Calculate protein cost for the amount you actually use. A break-even example shows how unfinished food changes a bulk package comparison.

By GetMacros · Published October 3, 2026

The protein-cost calculator compares the protein in a package with its price. For a household decision, also ask how much of that food you will use. Paying less per gram on the label can still cost more per gram eaten.

Use the package’s stated serving basis to calculate total protein. FDA’s label guide explains why serving amounts matter. The prices and use rates below are invented examples, not shopping quotes.

Small pack versus bulk pack

Small pack: $4 for 80 g total protein. Bulk pack: $7 for 160 g total protein. If all the food is used, the small pack costs $0.05 per gram; the bulk pack costs $0.04375 per gram.

The small pack’s cost per 25 g is $1.25 if it is fully used. The bulk pack reaches that same cost when you use 140 of its 160 g protein—87.5%. Using less makes the bulk option more expensive in this example.

Work out your own break-even point

Divide the larger pack’s price by the smaller pack’s cost per gram. That gives the protein you must use to match the smaller option. Then divide by the larger pack’s total protein.

Here: $7 ÷ $0.05 = 140 g. Then 140 ÷ 160 = 87.5%. This calculation assumes discarded portions have the same protein concentration as retained portions. It cannot estimate selectively discarded bones, skin or separated ingredients.

Use the result as one purchasing check

Compare prices in the same currency and account for delivery or membership costs if they change the purchase. Storage space, taste, portion convenience and your available cash also matter. A cheaper protein gram is not a complete measure of a food’s nutrition.

Compare the package prices, then write down a realistic use rate. If the edible weight is different from the purchased weight, check that basis first.
