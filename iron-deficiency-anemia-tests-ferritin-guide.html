Iron and blood health

Iron-Deficiency Anemia Tests and Ferritin Guide

Anemia means too little hemoglobin or too few healthy red blood cells; iron deficiency is one cause among many. Testing must establish both the anemia and why it happened.

Urgent symptoms: Chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, a racing heart at rest, vomiting blood, black stool or heavy uncontrolled bleeding needs urgent medical assessment.

The CBC identifies a blood pattern

A complete blood count includes hemoglobin, hematocrit, red-cell number and average cell size. Small red cells can suggest iron deficiency, but inherited traits and chronic disease can produce overlapping patterns.

Ferritin reflects stored iron—with caveats

Low ferritin strongly supports depleted iron stores. Inflammation, infection, liver disease and other conditions can raise ferritin, so a normal or high result does not always exclude iron-restricted red-cell production.

Transferrin measures add context

Serum iron changes throughout the day and is not interpreted alone. Transferrin, total iron-binding capacity and transferrin saturation help clinicians distinguish patterns.

Find the cause, not only the deficit

Heavy menstrual bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding, pregnancy, blood donation, growth, low intake, celiac disease, bariatric surgery and inflammatory disease can contribute. In adults without an obvious explanation, unexplained deficiency may require gastrointestinal evaluation.

Anemia of inflammation differs

Inflammation can trap iron in storage and reduce red-cell production even when tissue stores are not empty. Extra iron is not automatically the solution; treating the underlying condition matters.

Follow the trend

Response to treatment may be assessed with symptoms, hemoglobin and iron stores over time. Feeling better does not prove stores are restored, and a persistently abnormal result requires reassessment.

Bring useful details

Record menstrual or other bleeding, digestive symptoms, donations, medicines, supplements, pregnancy status, dietary pattern and family history. Those details are as important as the ferritin value.
