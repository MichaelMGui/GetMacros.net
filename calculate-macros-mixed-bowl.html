Build the meal total one ingredient at a time

Your shelf

Scale each ingredient to the amount in your bowl, then add calories and protein. A four-part example includes a sauce and prevents mixed-unit errors.

In this guide
A four-part bowlThree checks before addingIf one ingredient is missing data

By GetMacros · Published October 3, 2026

For each ingredient, calculate the portion multiplier, scale its nutrition, then add the results. The rice, protein food, vegetables and sauce each need their own serving basis.

This is a method for known labels or matching food entries. A meal name alone—“chicken bowl,” for example—does not reveal exact portions or ingredients. USDA FoodData Central documentation describes its different data types; an exact branded product and a generic food entry need not be interchangeable.

A four-part bowl

All figures below are hypothetical. They demonstrate scaling, not the nutrition of a named product or restaurant meal.

IngredientLabel basisAmount eatenCalories in bowlProtein in bowl
Cooked grain200 calories, 4 g protein per 150 g180 g2404.8 g
Prepared protein food150 calories, 25 g protein per 100 g120 g18030 g
Vegetables40 calories, 2 g protein per 100 g75 g301.5 g
Sauce60 calories, 0 g protein per 30 mL15 mL300 g
Total48036.3 g

For the grain, the multiplier is 180 ÷ 150 = 1.2. For the sauce, it is 15 ÷ 30 = 0.5. Do not use one multiplier for the entire bowl.

Three checks before adding

  1. Match each ingredient’s preparation: cooked grain values for cooked grain weight.
  2. Keep units together within each ingredient: mL divided by mL, g divided by g.
  3. Check whether oil, dressing or toppings are already included in a prepared ingredient’s value.

Once the portions have been scaled, calories add to calories and protein grams add to protein grams. You do not need to combine all the ingredient weights into a single serving definition.

If one ingredient is missing data

A missing protein value is not zero. Report the protein subtotal for known ingredients and state what is missing. Do not present that subtotal as a complete bowl total. When a manufacturer supplies the missing value, rerun the addition.

Compare two ingredient labels on the same basis, then divide a larger recipe if the bowl is part of a batch.
