Restaurant nutrition: choose the country where you order

Your shelf

Match the country, recipe and serving before comparing calories or protein.

In this guide
Match the record before comparingSource dates matter tooWhat to do when the market is unsupported

By GetMacros · Published October 4, 2026

Use the nutrition information for the market where you are ordering. A familiar restaurant name does not make portion sizes, standard ingredients or nutrient figures globally interchangeable. GetMacros separates its U.S. selection from its Canadian A&W, Harvey’s and Tim Hortons items. Choose the country in the header, or open the Canadian meal finder directly.

Match the record before comparing

Look for the country or region, the exact item name, serving size and included components. A similar menu title in another country may describe another build. A number from the other country is not a substitute when your local serving is unknown.

Source dates matter too

A newer page from another market is not automatically a better source for the old market. Check that the menu version and geographic scope both match. If a source does not state a publication date, keep that date unavailable and record when you consulted it separately.

What to do when the market is unsupported

Use the restaurant’s local official information for your order. You can compare those documented values yourself, but do not assume a GetMacros shortlist certifies the local meal. Shared GetMacros links and downloaded order cards identify the country so someone opening them can check the same serving.

FDA’s menu-labeling guidance is U.S.-specific context, not a global menu standard. This article introduces no cross-market nutrition claims and quotes no restaurant values. The practical rule is to keep brand, market and serving together whenever a number travels from a source to a result.

Canada’s row has 160 kcal more, but both its recipe and portion differ. Those values are not interchangeable, and their difference cannot identify the amount of an individual ingredient.

Choose your country first and then the exact menu family. A source’s separate turkey component must not be logged as the full sandwich. The source editions are deliberately retained; this is not evidence that a menu changed between those dates.
