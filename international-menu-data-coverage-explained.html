Restaurant nutrition by country: what the coverage counts mean

In this guide

By GetMacros · Published October 14, 2026 · Coverage updated October 17, 2026

A larger restaurant result count does not prove a country has more high-protein food. GetMacros tracks different source selections in each market: some include many individual components, others a small set of complete meals. Before comparing countries, separate coverage from the food itself.

“Tracked rows” includes recorded components. “Finder entries” excludes component and sauce rows and entries explicitly unavailable to the finder. “All four macros” means calories, protein, carbohydrate and fat are present; it does not certify current ingredients, allergens or every micronutrient. Fiber reported means a source value exists, including a reported zero.

Three decisions this changes

  1. Choose the country where you ordered. A bigger foreign catalogue is not a source for your local recipe.
  2. Check the fields you need. A calorie-only Pizza Nova serving cannot satisfy a verified protein constraint. McDonald’s Japan records lack fiber in the reviewed table; missing fiber is not a measured zero.
  3. Compare equivalent boundaries. A chicken component, a whole sandwich and a combo with a drink are different objects even when each occupies one row.

How to reproduce the snapshot

Counts come from the published restaurant-order registry grouped by market, with no weighting by population or store count. One source row is one record; calculated orders are still records. The figures measure this library’s coverage, not market share, consumer habits, national diets, or every restaurant operating in those countries.

For a real comparison, choose two or three exact orders and keep country, serving, sources and missing fields visible. A “best country” score would hide the sampling differences rather than answer your lunch question.
