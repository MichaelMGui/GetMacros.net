Hashimoto’s Diet: Gluten, Selenium and Common Myths

Hashimoto’s disease is autoimmune thyroid inflammation. No food plan has been shown to regenerate destroyed thyroid tissue or replace prescribed hormone.

Test before restricting gluten. Starting a gluten-free diet before celiac testing can make results harder to interpret. Ask a clinician when symptoms or family history warrant testing.

Gluten-free is not universal treatment

People with confirmed celiac disease need strict gluten avoidance. Without celiac disease or another diagnosed indication, evidence does not support gluten removal as a cure for Hashimoto’s.

Selenium has a narrow safety margin

Selenium is required for thyroid enzymes, but high-dose supplements can cause hair and nail changes, gastrointestinal symptoms and nerve problems. Brazil-nut content varies, so using handfuls as a dose is imprecise.

Iodine excess can worsen autoimmunity

Kelp, sea moss, dulse and iodine drops may contain large amounts. Adequacy matters, especially in pregnancy, but more is not better.

Use food to cover general needs

Protein foods, whole grains, produce, dairy or alternatives, nuts and seeds can support fibre, bone and cardiovascular health. Restriction can make adequacy and medication timing harder.

Antibody numbers are not the only outcome

A supplement may change an antibody marker without improving symptoms, thyroid function or long-term health. Ask whether a claimed benefit was clinically meaningful.

Persistent symptoms deserve a wider view

Anemia, B12 deficiency, sleep problems, depression, menopause and other autoimmune diseases may coexist. Do not funnel every symptom into an expanding avoid list.

Track what changes

If testing one dietary adjustment, keep it time-limited, change one variable and protect nutritional adequacy. Review medication and labs with the care team.
