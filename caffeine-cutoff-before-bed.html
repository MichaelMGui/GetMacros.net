Caffeine before bed: half-life, timing, and a personal cutoff

The coffee may be gone from the cup while a meaningful amount of caffeine remains in the body.

By the GetMacros.net editorial team · Reviewed August 13, 2026 · General education, not individualized sports or medical advice

Quick answer: Understand caffeine’s long and variable half-life, how late intake can affect sleep, and how to test a cutoff that fits your sensitivity.

Half-life is not a timer

Caffeine’s half-life is often measured in hours and varies with genetics, pregnancy, smoking, medications, liver function, and habitual use. A six-hour rule can be a starting experiment, not a promise that everyone clears caffeine on the same schedule.

Sleep effects can be subtle

Caffeine may delay sleep, reduce total sleep, or change sleep depth even when someone feels able to fall asleep. Tolerance to the alert feeling does not guarantee the sleep effect has disappeared.

Run a simple experiment

Keep wake time stable and move the final caffeine earlier for one or two weeks. Track sleep onset, awakenings, morning alertness, and afternoon energy. Include coffee, tea, energy drinks, pre-workout products, chocolate, and some medicines.
