Restaurant comparison

Which fast-food restaurant fits your goal?

“Healthiest” is too vague to order dinner. A better comparison asks what you need from the meal—and keeps the trade-offs visible.

15 chainsin the GetMacros finder83 orderswith named standard buildsAug 2026data review

Published August 27, 2026 · 10 minute read · By the GetMacros editorial team

The short answer

No chain wins every category. Chick-fil-A makes a lean, high-protein order unusually easy. Chipotle gives you the most control over a complete bowl. Subway scales cleanly from a lighter six-inch sandwich to a serious footlong. Panera has useful soup-and-salad combinations, while Taco Bell can work well when you want smaller pieces you can combine. For a large bulking meal, Panda Express and the biggest sandwich orders offer more energy—but sodium rises quickly.

Why one “healthiest restaurant” ranking fails

A 200-calorie box of grilled nuggets and a 650-calorie rice-and-bean bowl solve different problems. The nuggets are extremely protein-efficient, but many people need a side or another food for carbohydrate, fiber and enough energy. The bowl is a complete meal, but its standard sauces and seasoned ingredients bring much more sodium. Calling one healthy and the other unhealthy hides the useful information.

GetMacros compares named orders, not restaurant reputations. A chain can have one excellent high-protein item and many poor fits for the same goal. It can also offer a very large meal that is useful for someone intentionally eating 3,000 calories per day but unhelpful for someone seeking a modest lunch.

Best for high protein without many calories: Chick-fil-A

The 12-count grilled nuggets provide 200 calories and 38 grams of protein in the current tracked data. That is an unusually high amount of protein for the energy—but it is closer to a protein component than a balanced meal. Pairing it with fruit, a vegetable side or another carbohydrate may make more sense than treating “lowest calorie” as the finish line.

A useful order

12-count grilled nuggets: 200 calories, 38 g protein and 660 mg sodium. Sauces are not included. If you add one, count it as part of the order rather than an invisible extra.

If you want breakfast, the Egg White Grill is a more complete 290-calorie order with 26 grams of protein. The grilled chicken sandwich is easier to eat as a conventional meal: 390 calories and 28 grams of protein.

Best for building a complete bowl: Chipotle

Chipotle is the easiest chain in this comparison to adjust one meal component at a time. You can change the protein, rice, beans, vegetables and toppings independently. That flexibility is valuable, but it also means a nutrition number only describes the exact build attached to it.

The tracked High Protein–High Fiber Bowl has 540 calories, 46 grams of protein and 14 grams of fiber. Its 1,645 mg of sodium is the trade-off. A steak salad with black beans and no rice is lighter at 345 calories with 31 grams of protein and 13 grams of fiber, although it still has 1,255 mg sodium.

What makes the chain useful

Chipotle is not automatically “healthier.” It is legible: the ingredients are visible and the official calculator lets you test a precise custom build. See the complete Chipotle comparison before changing toppings.

Best for scaling the same idea up or down: Subway

A sandwich format makes portion scaling intuitive. A six-inch rotisserie chicken sub in the tracked data has 320 calories and 29 grams of protein; the footlong version has 640 calories and 58 grams. That does not make every footlong exactly double after custom cheese, sauce or bread changes, but the standard build offers a clear starting point.

For larger appetites, a footlong can be a practical high-energy meal rather than a mistake. For a lighter lunch, a six-inch sandwich with extra vegetables may be enough. Sodium remains the main number to watch across many deli-meat and restaurant sandwich builds.

Best for fiber in a compact hot meal: Panera

Panera’s turkey chili illustrates why a meal cannot be judged by protein alone: the tracked bowl has 340 calories, 26 grams of protein and 11 grams of fiber. At 1,140 mg sodium, it is not low-sodium, but it gives you more fiber than many meat-led fast-food meals.

Half portions can also be useful here. A smaller soup, salad or sandwich component lets you build a meal around appetite instead of choosing between “full entrée” and “nothing.” Check the current menu because availability changes more frequently than a permanent burger or taco.

Best for mixing small items: Taco Bell

A chicken soft taco is a 170-calorie building block in the tracked data. One might be a snack; two or three can become a meal. The chicken Power Menu Bowl is more complete at 470 calories, 26 grams of protein and 8 grams of fiber. The vegetarian bowl has less protein but 11 grams of fiber.

This modularity is helpful for people whose appetite does not match a standard combo. The catch is that add-ons and sauces can change the total faster than the base item suggests.

Best for a serious bulking order: Panda Express

Someone eating around 3,000 calories per day may genuinely want a restaurant meal near 1,000 calories. GetMacros tracks a Panda Express Bigger Plate with fried rice and three grilled teriyaki chicken entrées at 1,445 calories and 112 grams of protein. That is a very large order, not a recommendation for everyone. It also has 2,410 mg sodium before extra sauce or a drink.

Large Chick-fil-A and Subway orders can also cross 1,000 calories with substantial protein. The right question is not whether that is “too much” in isolation. It is whether the portion is intentional, how it fits the whole day, and how you feel eating it.

How to choose in 30 seconds

  1. Name the job. Is this a light lunch, post-training meal, high-calorie dinner, vegetarian option or simply the food you want?
  2. Choose a calorie range. A range is more realistic than a perfect number.
  3. Set one or two priorities. High protein plus high fiber is useful. Trying to optimize calories, protein, carbs, fat, sodium and cost at once usually creates a joyless order.
  4. Look at the complete meal. Include sauce, dressing, sides and drinks.
  5. Confirm current data. Recipes and menus change. Use the restaurant’s own nutrition page when accuracy matters.

Healthy Order Match lets you combine goals such as high protein and bulking, choose a calorie range, and compare five best matches before opening more results.

Method and limitations

This comparison uses the named standard U.S. menu builds in GetMacros’ August 2026 restaurant dataset. Values come from official chain nutrition pages or ingredient tables linked on each chain guide. Missing fiber or sodium stays missing instead of being treated as zero. Restaurant portions vary, limited-time menus change and customization alters the result. The ranking is a decision aid, not medical advice or an endorsement of any chain.

For the freshest source trail, open the relevant restaurant nutrition guide. If sodium, allergens or another nutrient has medical importance for you, confirm the current order with the restaurant and your clinician.

Bottom line

Pick the restaurant that makes your next decision easiest. High protein favors Chick-fil-A’s grilled items. A complete customizable meal favors Chipotle. Portion scaling favors Subway. Fiber can favor Panera or a bean-based bowl. A serious bulk can favor Panda Express. None wins every day—and none needs to.
