Choose a starting format

A salad base usually leaves more room for dips, dressings or a pita; a grains bowl makes carbohydrate a larger part of the meal; a greens-and-grains split provides both. None is automatically best. Athletes or people with larger appetites may welcome rice and pita, while someone seeking a smaller lunch may prefer greens, one dip and a measured dressing.

Options for different dietary patterns

  • Higher protein: start with chicken or steak, then use the live builder to check the complete total. Lentils add plant protein and fibre.
  • Vegetarian: falafel, lentils, hummus and vegetables can form a substantial bowl. Dairy-containing feta, Crazy Feta and yogurt sauces need attention.
  • Plant-based: begin with greens, grains or lentils, falafel, hummus and vegetables; exclude dairy sauces and cheese. Recheck every ingredient because recipes change.
  • Gluten-aware: a bowl without pita or pita crisps may avoid obvious wheat ingredients, but shared preparation means it is not a celiac-safety guarantee.
  • Lower sodium: dips, pickles, cheese and dressing can stack. Choosing fewer salty extras and using dressing lightly is more reliable than judging by calories.

Complete-meal math

A 580-calorie Greek Salad plus a 320-calorie side pita becomes a 900-calorie order before a drink. Conversely, removing part of a standard recipe can reduce energy but also flavour and satisfaction. Count the components you actually eat and avoid treating a side as nutritionally invisible.

Allergy boundary

CAVA states that shared kitchen preparation prevents it from guaranteeing an allergen-free item. Tell staff about an allergy and use the current allergen guide; a list of ingredients on a menu card is not a cross-contact assessment.
