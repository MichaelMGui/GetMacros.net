Migraine nutrition

Migraine Hydration and Electrolytes

Dehydration can contribute to headache for some people, but not every migraine means a fluid or electrolyte deficit.

Safety boundary: Confusion, seizure, fainting, severe vomiting or inability to keep fluids down needs prompt care. Excess water can also cause dangerous low sodium.

Use a repeatable baseline

Drink regularly with meals and according to thirst, heat, activity, illness and individual medical guidance. Urine colour is a rough clue, not a hydration laboratory.

Electrolytes are situational

Long sweaty activity, significant vomiting or diarrhea, and some heat exposures may warrant sodium-containing fluid. Ordinary desk days do not automatically require a powder.

Read the whole label

Products vary in sodium, potassium, sugar, caffeine and sweeteners. High-dose potassium can be unsafe with kidney disease or certain medicines. “Sugar-free” does not mean medically superior.

Do not chase pain with litres

Once adequately hydrated, more water is not established migraine treatment. Rapid overdrinking can dilute blood sodium.

Look beyond fluids

Sleep loss, caffeine withdrawal, missed meals, medication overuse and an evolving illness may accompany headache. Use the hydration collection for deeper safety guidance.
