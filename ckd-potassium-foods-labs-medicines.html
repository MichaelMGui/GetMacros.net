Kidney-health nutrition

CKD Potassium: Foods, Labs and Medicines

Potassium supports nerves, muscles and heart rhythm. With CKD, levels may become too high—but not everyone with kidney disease needs a low-potassium diet.

High potassium can be silent and dangerous. Palpitations, severe weakness, chest symptoms or fainting need urgent assessment, but only a blood test establishes the level.

Interpret a result with context

Kidney function, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, supplements, salt substitutes, constipation, diabetes and sample handling can affect potassium. Never stop medicine without prescriber guidance.

Lists are a starting point, not a prescription

Portion, preparation and frequency matter. A renal dietitian can identify the largest contributors while preserving fibre and food variety. Eliminating fruits, vegetables, beans and dairy indiscriminately can create deficiencies and poor intake.

Salt substitutes deserve special attention

Many replace sodium chloride with potassium chloride. They may look heart healthy but can be unsafe in CKD or with certain medicines. Photograph the ingredient list for the clinician or pharmacist.

Food labels are incomplete

Potassium must appear in Nutrition Facts for many packaged foods, yet restaurant and unlabelled foods remain uncertain. Ingredient lists can reveal potassium additives, but the clinical significance depends on the full diet and laboratory trend.

Preparation can change potassium

Boiling and discarding water can lower potassium in some vegetables, but the effect varies. Do not rely on improvised leaching to make an unrestricted portion safe; use instructions from a renal dietitian.

Dialysis changes the plan

Dialysis type, frequency and residual kidney function affect removal. Home or more frequent dialysis may allow different choices from conventional hemodialysis. Update the food plan when modality changes.

Ask three useful questions

  1. Is my potassium actually high, low or stable?
  2. Which foods, supplements or medicines are the largest contributors for me?
  3. What symptoms and result thresholds require urgent action?
