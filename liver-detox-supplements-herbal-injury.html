Liver-health nutrition

Liver Detox Supplements and Herbal Injury

The liver already transforms and removes substances through complex pathways. A tea, juice or supplement cannot rinse it clean—and some “detox” products can directly injure it.

Stop and seek care for jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, severe itching, persistent vomiting, unusual fatigue or right-upper abdominal pain after starting a product. Bring the container.

Natural does not mean liver-safe

Plants contain pharmacologically active chemicals. Kava and other ingredients have been linked with liver injury, and multi-ingredient products make the culprit difficult to identify.

Marketing words are not evidence

“Cleanse,” “standardized,” “clinically studied” and “doctor formulated” do not prove effectiveness, identity or dose consistency. Testimonials cannot establish safety.

Bodybuilding and weight-loss products deserve scrutiny

Some contain undeclared drugs, stimulants or anabolic compounds. Rapid results and proprietary blends are warning signs. Third-party certification can reduce contamination risk but cannot prove that the product benefits the liver.

Green tea as a drink differs from extracts

A normal beverage and a concentrated capsule are not equivalent exposures. Extracts can deliver much higher doses and have been associated with liver injury in susceptible people.

Interactions extend beyond the liver

Supplements can alter blood thinners, transplant medicines, antidepressants and many other treatments. Share exact product names and photos with a pharmacist or clinician.

Do not use detox fasting

Juice fasts can provide inadequate protein, cause glucose problems and worsen malnutrition in advanced liver disease. Treating the cause, stopping harmful exposure safely and maintaining nutrition are the real priorities.

Report suspected injury

Clinicians may ask when the product started, dose, lot number and other medicines. Keep the packaging. In the United States, adverse events can also be reported through FDA safety systems.
