Jersey Mike’s Balanced Subs and Macros

At Jersey Mike’s, size and preparation style can change the meal more than the sub number. A Mini, Regular, Giant and Sub in a Tub are not interchangeable entries, and “Mike’s Way” adds oil, vinegar and seasonings that belong in the total.

↗Cutting, maintaining or bulking?

Choose the portion for your energy needs. For a higher-calorie meal, add a useful side, grain, bread, dressing or larger entrée while keeping protein and overall meal quality in view. For a calorie deficit, use a smaller portion—not an automatically “better” food.

A balanced turkey build

Start with the #7 Turkey and Provolone in the size that matches your hunger. Keep lettuce, tomato, onion, peppers and vinegar for volume and flavour. Then decide whether you want the oil blend, cheese and mayonnaise rather than removing everything automatically. Enter the exact build in the official calculator and save that result with your food log.

Options for different dietary patterns

  • Higher protein: turkey, roast beef, tuna and chicken-based subs can contribute meaningful protein. Compare sodium and preparation, not protein alone.
  • Vegetarian: the #14 Veggie contains cheese in its standard identity. Confirm bread, cheese and topping ingredients.
  • Vegan: options are limited and location-dependent. A vegetable build without cheese or mayonnaise still requires bread and cross-contact verification.
  • Lower carbohydrate: Sub in a Tub removes bread, but the resulting sodium, fat and vegetable balance still depends on fillings and dressings.
  • Gluten-aware: gluten-free bread availability does not make a shared sub-preparation line celiac-safe. Ask about the location’s procedures.
  • Lower sodium: processed meat, cheese, pickles and seasonings add up. A smaller size and fewer high-sodium additions may help, but check the calculated total.

The giant-size trap

A Giant is designed as a much larger portion. If you intend to save half, separate it before eating and refrigerate promptly; then log the amount eaten. “One sandwich” is not a useful serving description when sizes differ so much.

Allergy and ingredient boundary

Cheese, tuna, bread, sauces and shared equipment introduce milk, fish, wheat, egg and cross-contact questions. The official ingredient and allergen resources should decide suitability, not a generic diet label on a third-party page.
