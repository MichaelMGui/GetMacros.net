Grams and milliliters are different measurements

Your shelf

A practical way to compare labels in grams and milliliters without assuming every liquid weighs the same. Includes a worked dressing example.

In this guide
Choose the comparison you can supportA dressing exampleKeep the unit in your notes

By GetMacros · Published October 3, 2026

Use grams with grams and milliliters with milliliters. If one label uses weight and the other uses volume, you need a product-specific conversion before comparing them. Changing the unit name in a calculator does not convert the food.

A gram measures mass; a milliliter measures volume. NIST’s mass definitions and volume guide explain the distinction. Foods need not have the same mass for the same volume.

A dressing example

These invented figures illustrate the arithmetic, not the nutrition of a real dressing. Suppose a product label explicitly gives 30 mL as 36 g and lists 120 calories for that serving. You want to use 20 g of that same product.

20 ÷ 36 × 120 = about 67 calories. The label’s own weight is enough to scale the portion. You do not need to turn 20 g into mL first. If you did need the volume, this example gives 20 ÷ 36 × 30 = about 16.7 mL.

That conversion belongs to this hypothetical product. Do not reuse it for another dressing, oil or yogurt. If a label does not provide both units, measure the portion in the unit it uses or ask the manufacturer.

Keep the unit in your notes

Write “20 g dressing” or “20 mL dressing,” rather than just “20 dressing.” The food comparison tool uses grams. Use it when both label serving weights are available in grams. For mL-only labels, do the volume calculation above or get a product-specific gram conversion; never put a milliliter figure into a field labeled grams. Comparing equal weights answers a different question from comparing your two chosen portions.
