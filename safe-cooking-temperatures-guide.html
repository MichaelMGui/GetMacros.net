Safe Cooking Temperatures: Where and How to Use a Food Thermometer

Colour, clear juices, and cooking time are unreliable safety tests. Put a thermometer in the food, not faith in the recipe photo.

U.S. household guidance: These pages use USDA, FDA, and FoodSafety.gov recommendations. Local rules may differ. Pregnant people, adults 65 and older, children under 5, and people with weakened immune systems face higher foodborne-illness risk and may need stricter choices.

The core U.S. temperature chart

  • Poultry, stuffing, casseroles, and leftovers: 165°F (74°C).
  • Ground beef, pork, lamb, and veal: 160°F (71°C).
  • Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, lamb, bison, and goat: 145°F (63°C), followed by a three-minute rest.
  • Fish with fins: 145°F (63°C), or until flesh is opaque and separates easily where a thermometer cannot be used effectively.
  • Egg dishes: 160°F (71°C); cook eggs until yolk and white are firm unless using a pasteurized product in a recipe intended otherwise.

Always check the current FoodSafety.gov chart because food categories and instructions can be more specific than a short summary.

Place the thermometer correctly

Insert it into the thickest part away from bone, fat, and gristle. For thin foods such as burgers or chicken breasts, insert from the side so the sensing area reaches the centre. Check multiple spots in large, irregular, or microwave-cooked foods.

Know your thermometer’s sensing area

Some instant-read thermometers sense near the tip; dial thermometers may average temperature across a longer stem and need deeper insertion. Read the manufacturer instructions. Calibrate as recommended and replace a damaged or unreliable device.

Rest time is part of the safety instruction

For whole cuts listed at 145°F, the three-minute rest allows temperature effects to continue before carving or eating. Do not apply that temperature-and-rest combination to ground meat or poultry; their required minimums are different.

Reheating is not initial cooking

Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). Bring sauces, soups, and gravies to a rolling boil. In a microwave, cover, rotate or stir, allow standing time, and check several locations because cold spots can remain.

Clean between checks

Wash the thermometer probe with hot, soapy water before and after use, especially when checking raw food and then checking again later. Do not let the tool that verifies safety become a cross-contamination route.

Colour can mislead

A burger may brown before reaching a safe internal temperature, while safely cooked poultry can remain pink near bone. A thermometer answers the relevant question directly.

Kitchen habit: Know the category, insert correctly, check more than one spot, respect rest time, and clean the probe.
