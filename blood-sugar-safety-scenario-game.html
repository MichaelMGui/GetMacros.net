How it works

Check your reasoning about labels, meals, low glucose, exercise, monitoring and emergencies. Answer each prompt, read why the answer is what it is, and move on. There is no timer, no sign-up and nothing is saved — you can restart whenever you like.

What this covers

Each round gives you a claim to judge, then explains the reasoning behind the answer rather than just marking it right or wrong. Statements you will weigh up include:

  • For carbohydrate counting, which label line is the starting point?
  • Total sugars","Total carbohydrate
  • A food has 30 g carbohydrate per serving. You eat 1.5 servings. Total?
  • 20 g","30 g","45 g"],2,"Multiply the label value by the servings actually eaten: 30 × 1.5 = 45 g."],["Does diabetes require eliminating all carbohydrate?
  • An alert adult has glucose below their low threshold. The plan usually prioritizes:
  • Fast-acting carbohydrate and rechecking
  • An unconscious person with suspected hypoglycemia should receive juice by mouth.
  • Can exercise cause delayed low glucose with insulin?

Why bother

Recognising a shaky nutrition claim is a more durable skill than memorising a food list, because the claims change every year and the reasoning does not. If you would rather read first, browse the guide library or try a quiz.
