What to eat before a workout

A pre-workout meal should support the session without becoming the session’s main challenge.

By the GetMacros.net editorial team · Reviewed August 13, 2026 · General education, not individualized sports or medical advice

Quick answer: Plan food before training using time available, session demands, digestion, and personal tolerance rather than one universal rule.

Timing changes the meal

A meal three or four hours before training can be larger and more mixed. Food within an hour is usually smaller and easier to digest. The closer exercise gets, the more useful familiar, lower-fiber, lower-fat carbohydrate choices may become for people prone to gastrointestinal discomfort.

Match the session

A short easy workout may require no special meal. Long endurance training, high-volume lifting, or multiple daily sessions can make pre-exercise carbohydrate more relevant. Protein in a prior meal can contribute amino acids, but there is no need to force a shake immediately before every workout.

Test in training

Tolerance varies. Some people train well after breakfast; others prefer more time. Practice the exact food and timing before competition. New supplements, unusually high fiber, large portions, and heavy fried meals add avoidable uncertainty.
