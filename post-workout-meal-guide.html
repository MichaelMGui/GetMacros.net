Performance and recovery

What to eat after a workout

Recovery nutrition matters, but the body does not stop adapting because a shake arrived a few minutes late.

By the GetMacros.net editorial team · Reviewed August 13, 2026 · General education, not individualized sports or medical advice

Quick answer: Build a useful recovery meal with protein, carbohydrate, fluid, and enough total energy—without treating a narrow window as an emergency.

Protein supports remodeling

A meaningful serving of high-quality protein after training can support muscle protein synthesis. Total daily protein and distribution across meals remain important, so the post-workout meal should fit the whole day rather than sit outside it.

Carbohydrate restores fuel

Carbohydrate helps replenish glycogen. Urgency rises when another demanding session is coming within hours; a recreational lifter with a full day before the next workout has more flexibility.

Build an actual meal

Combine a protein food, carbohydrate-rich food, and produce, then add fats and flavor according to appetite. Yogurt with fruit and oats, rice with tofu and vegetables, eggs with toast, or a sandwich and milk can all work. Supplements are convenience, not a requirement.

What recovery actually requires

Three things are being replaced after a hard session: muscle protein, glycogen and fluid. Only the last is urgent.

TargetHow muchHow urgent
Protein0.3–0.4 g/kg (20–30 g)Within a few hours
Carbohydrate0.5–1.2 g/kg after long sessionsWithin hours; sooner if training again today
Fluid125–150% of weight lostPromptly
SodiumWith the fluid, if sweating was heavyPromptly

For a 70 kg person that is roughly 25 g of protein and, after a genuinely long session, 35–85 g of carbohydrate. After an hour of lifting, the carbohydrate figure is at the very bottom of that range or unnecessary.

The window is hours, not minutes

The 30-minute anabolic window came from studies comparing post-workout protein against nothing at all. When later work controlled for total daily protein, the timing advantage mostly disappeared. If you ate a protein-containing meal within a few hours of training, sprinting to a shaker is solving a problem you do not have.

Two genuine exceptions: training fasted, where the last feeding is far behind, and training twice in one day, where glycogen has to be restored quickly for the second session. Outside those, eat a normal meal when you are hungry and hit your daily totals.
