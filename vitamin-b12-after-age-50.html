Vitamin B12 after age 50: food, fortified sources, absorption, and testing

Getting enough B12 on paper and absorbing enough B12 are not always the same problem. Reduced stomach acid can make naturally food-bound B12 harder to release.

Original GetMacros.net editorial guide · Updated August 13, 2026

The two-step absorption process

Stomach acid helps release vitamin B12 from protein in food. The freed vitamin then binds intrinsic factor before absorption in the small intestine. With age, atrophic gastritis and lower stomach acidity become more common, so the first step can become less efficient.

B12 in fortified foods and supplements is not attached to food protein and does not require that release step, although normal intestinal absorption still matters.

Food and fortified sources

Fish, meat, poultry, eggs, milk, and other dairy foods naturally contain B12. Fortified cereals, nutritional yeast, and some plant beverages can provide B12 when the label confirms it. Product formulations change, so check the current serving and percent Daily Value.

Who needs closer assessment

Risk can rise with pernicious anemia, stomach or intestinal surgery, celiac or Crohn’s disease, long-term use of certain acid-suppressing medicines or metformin, and eating little or no animal food without reliable fortification. NIH notes that B12 deficiency affects a meaningful share of older adults.

Symptoms cannot diagnose it

Deficiency can cause anemia, fatigue, weakness, numbness, balance problems, memory or mood changes, and tongue soreness. Neurological injury can occur even without obvious anemia. These symptoms overlap with many conditions; testing and interpretation are necessary.

Supplements are not all absorbed equally

Very high oral doses can allow some passive absorption, but treatment depends on cause and severity. Pernicious anemia or significant neurological symptoms require medical management. “Methyl” versus “cyano” marketing should not distract from dose, diagnosis, adherence, and follow-up testing.

Build a useful B12 conversation

Bring the clinician a list of animal foods, fortified products, and supplements used during a typical week. Photograph labels when brands matter. Include metformin, acid-suppressing medicines, and any history of stomach or intestinal surgery, since diet alone may not explain status.

Testing can involve serum B12 and, in selected situations, markers such as methylmalonic acid. Kidney function and other factors can affect interpretation, so one online cutoff should not replace clinical context. Folate can improve the anemia of B12 deficiency while neurological injury continues, which is another reason to avoid self-treatment based only on a blood count.

If a fortified cereal or beverage is the main source, check how often it is actually consumed and whether the serving matches the label. A high percentage on a product used once a month is less reliable than a modest source eaten consistently. For vegan older adults, plan dependable fortified or supplemental B12 rather than relying on unfortified fermented foods or algae claims.

Primary reference

NIH National Institute on Aging or Office of Dietary Supplements
