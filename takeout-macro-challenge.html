The reasoning behind the score

Three rules stop bad comparisons.

01

Compare like with like

A side, entrée and complete meal serve different jobs. Apple slices cannot fairly “beat” a grain bowl merely because the calorie number is lower.

02

Use the actual goal

Higher protein plus higher energy is different from a lighter lunch plus fibre. A useful recommendation begins by naming what this meal needs to do.

03

Keep safety separate

Plant-based and gluten-aware labels are useful filters, but food allergies and celiac disease require current ingredients and cross-contact information.

A 30-second method

Five questions before you order.

Use these after the quiz whenever a menu has too many numbers and too little context.

1

What is this meal for?

Name the job: a lighter snack, a filling lunch, recovery after training, higher energy for bulking, or simply something enjoyable that fits the day.

2

Is it a full order?

Compare everything you expect to eat or drink. A small entrée, side or sauce should not be presented as though it were the entire meal.

3

Does the portion fit?

Calories are not a score. A lower number can be too little for one person, while a higher-energy meal may better match appetite, training and goals.

4

What is missing?

Look beyond protein. Fibre, produce, fluids, sodium and total energy can change how useful an order is. Keep unpublished values marked as unknown.

5

What needs verification?

Recipes and availability change. Check the restaurant’s current nutrition and allergen information, especially for allergies, celiac disease or medically directed limits.
