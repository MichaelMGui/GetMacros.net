Food labels

Use the column that matches your preparation

Avoid counting milk or oil twice. Check what an as-prepared column includes and adjust a worked example when your preparation differs.

In this guide
Count the added milk onceIf you use a different amount or productPreparation notes that deserve a second look

By GetMacros · Published October 3, 2026

“As packaged” describes the product before your preparation. “As prepared” describes it made according to the stated instructions. Read the footnote: the prepared column may already include milk, oil or other ingredients.

FDA describes this distinction in its Food Labeling Guide. The useful question at home is simple: did you make the food the way that column assumes?

Count the added milk once

Imagine a cereal label with the following values. They are hypothetical and do not describe a specific cereal or milk.

Nutrition basisCaloriesProtein
40 g cereal alone1504 g
Specified 150 mL milk755 g
Cereal with that milk2259 g

Use either the 225-calorie prepared column, or add the 150-calorie cereal and 75-calorie milk yourself. Using 225 and adding the milk again gives 300 calories, counting the same milk twice.

If you use a different amount or product

Suppose you use 200 mL of a different drink whose own label lists 40 calories and 2 g protein per 100 mL. That drink contributes 80 calories and 4 g protein. Added to the cereal, the total is 230 calories and 8 g protein.

The original prepared column no longer describes your bowl. Start with the cereal-only value and your actual added drink. Keep the drink’s milliliters separate from the cereal’s grams; add the resulting nutrients, not the portion numbers.

Preparation notes that deserve a second look

  • A dry mix that is prepared with butter or oil.
  • A soup that assumes a particular dilution.
  • A meal kit with separate sauce or seasoning packets.
  • A label that gives nutrition for the whole recipe rather than one plate.

If you discard a component, use its separately published nutrition when available. Do not assume every packet contributes an equal share of the total. If the manufacturer only provides combined values, the exact modified recipe may remain unknown.

Add separate food labels into one meal without mixing up their serving bases.
