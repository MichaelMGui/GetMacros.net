Choline: what it does and where to find it

Choline is an essential nutrient used to build cell membranes and make acetylcholine, an important neurotransmitter.

Home › Micronutrient library › Choline: what it does and where to find it

By the GetMacros.net editorial team · Reviewed August 13, 2026 · Educational content, not medical advice

Quick answer: Learn how choline supports cell membranes, neurotransmitter production, and liver function, with practical food sources and reference values.

What it does

The body produces some choline, but not always enough to meet needs. Choline also participates in lipid transport and methyl-group metabolism. Adequate Intakes differ by age, sex, pregnancy, and lactation.

Food sources and absorption

Eggs, meat, fish, dairy, soybeans, kidney beans, and some cruciferous vegetables contribute. People can obtain choline from mixed, vegetarian, or vegan patterns, but the food combinations differ.

Safety and individual context

High supplemental intakes can cause adverse effects. Food-first planning is usually the simplest starting point. Pregnancy and liver disease are good reasons to discuss individual needs with a qualified professional.

Primary reference

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements consumer fact sheet

How to use this information

Reference values describe population needs and are not a diagnosis. Food patterns, health conditions, pregnancy, laboratory results, and medications can change what is appropriate. Avoid high-dose supplementation unless there is a clear reason and qualified guidance.
